# Boilerplate library, reviewer read-through, and submission

## Boilerplate library with freshness checks

An approved boilerplate library saves time only if its facts are current. Keep each block with its metadata:

| Block | Last approved | Approved by | Facts inside that change | Freshness rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mission and history | Date | Executive director | Founding year, program list | Review yearly |
| Organizational capacity | Date | Executive director | Staff count, budget size, partners, results | Review each quarter or when any fact changes |
| Leadership bios | Date | Each person | Titles, qualifications | Review when roles change |
| Board list | Date | Board chair | Members, affiliations | Review after each board election |
| Financial summary | Date | Finance lead | Revenue mix, audit status | Review after each audit or close of fiscal year |
| Equity and inclusion statement | Date | Leadership | Commitments, practices | Review yearly |

(Freshness rules are illustrative defaults for the user to set.)

Before reusing a block:

1. Check its last-approved date against the rule.
2. Check every number and name inside it against the current source (staff roster, latest financials, program data).
3. Mark the block "Current" or "Update needed" and list the specific facts that changed.
4. Adapt the length and emphasis to the funder's question, without changing approved claims. If a change alters a claim, route it for re-approval.

Rewording approved text for length is usually fine; adding a new claim, number, or story is not, and needs approval from whoever owns the block.

## Reviewer read-through

Read the draft as the funder's reviewer, with the scoring rubric beside it. For each criterion:

| Criterion | Points | Where addressed | Reviewer's likely reading | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Questions to ask of each section:

- Does it answer the question asked, in the first two sentences?
- Would a reviewer find the evidence for each claim without searching?
- Does it use the funder's language for its priorities, where honest?
- Are the numbers consistent with other sections and the budget?
- Is anything promised that the program cannot control?
- Is there any identifiable participant information, unsuppressed small group, or story without recorded consent?
- Does any mental-health content imply clinical outcomes or break safe messaging practice?

Be candid in the "Reviewer's likely reading" column. A section that would score low should say so; the user benefits more from an honest read now than from a decline later.

Fix the lowest-scoring, highest-point sections first.

## Final submission checklist

Present as a table, not checkboxes.

| Item | Requirement source | Owner | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All narrative sections within limits | Compliance matrix | Writer | |
| Attachments complete and named per instructions | Attachments list | Grants lead | |
| Budget matches narrative and funder template | Budget instructions | Finance lead | |
| Authorized signer has reviewed and approved | Organization policy | Executive director | |
| Portal registration and account access confirmed | Submission instructions | Grants lead | |
| Deadline and time zone confirmed; submit at least a day early | Key dates | Grants lead | |
| Confirmation of submission saved | Submission instructions | Grants lead | |

After submission, record the application in the CRM with date, amount requested, and decision date, and move the prospect to the Decision stage (research-grant-prospects).

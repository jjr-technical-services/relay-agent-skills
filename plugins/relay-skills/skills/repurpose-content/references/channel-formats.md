# Channel formats and accessibility

Channel conventions change often. Treat the patterns below as commonly used starting
points and follow the organization's own style guide and each platform's current limits.

## Per-asset patterns

| Asset | Structure that works | Fidelity risk to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Newsletter section | Headline, one-sentence finding with base, why it matters to this reader, one action, link to full source | Headline paraphrase widening the finding |
| Instagram carousel | Slide 1: the finding in plain words; one claim per slide; base and source on each data slide; final slide with call to action | Base dropped from later slides |
| LinkedIn post | Opening line with the most relevant finding for professionals, two or three short paragraphs, source named, link in post or first comment per house style | Hook written as cause |
| Short video or reel | One finding, spoken and on-screen text; burned-in or platform captions | Captions that paraphrase the speaker |
| Blog post | Summary paragraph, findings with base, method box (who, how many, when, how), what the organization is doing, link to report | Adding interpretation the report did not make |
| Talking points | Three to five points, each with the approved sentence, a plain version, and the claim ID; a "do not say" line for known drift | Speakers improvising outcomes |
| Slide | One takeaway headline that is itself a supported claim, chart with base in caption, source line | Chart axes or rounding changed |
| Email to partners | Why this matters to them, two findings, ask, link | New audience or co-brand trigger |
| Partner kit | Approved copy blocks per channel, image files with alt text, usage rules, "what not to change" list, contact for questions | Partners editing claims; publishing in their voice |

## Partner kits

A partner kit is always New claim or audience on first release because another
organization publishes it. Include:

- Ready-to-post copy for each channel, each with claim IDs listed in a notes column.
- A short "you may change" list (greeting, sign-off, internal channel name) and a "do not
  change" list (numbers, bases, population, caveats, crisis resource wording).
- Alt text for every image and captions for every video.
- The organization-approved call to action and link, and the review contact.
- For internal employee channels, note that employees may be affected by the topic;
  include the `ORGANIZATION-APPROVED CRISIS RESOURCE (insert approved wording)` slot when the content touches
  mental-health crises, and route the kit through write-safe-mental-health-content.

## Accessibility requirements

- **Alt text**: describe what the image shows and its purpose in one or two sentences;
  for a chart, state the takeaway and the key numbers with their base. Do not start
  with "image of". Decorative images get empty alt text where the platform allows.
- **Captions**: every video needs accurate captions; auto-captions must be corrected by
  a person before publishing. Provide a transcript for long video or audio.
- **Hashtags**: write multi-word hashtags in capital-case per word so screen readers
  can separate words, and place them at the end.
- **Emoji**: if house style allows them, use few, never in place of words, and not in
  the middle of sentences, because screen readers read each one aloud.
- **Links**: descriptive link text ("read the full report"), not "click here".
- **Colour and contrast**: text on images needs strong contrast; do not rely on colour
  alone to carry meaning in charts.
- **Plain language**: define any term a general reader might not know on first use.

## Sequencing

Publish the fullest version (blog or report page) first so every short asset can link
to the source with its method. Then release shorter assets. Partner kits go last, after
full review.

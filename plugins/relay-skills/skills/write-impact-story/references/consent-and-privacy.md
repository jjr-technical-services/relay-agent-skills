# Consent and privacy

## Consent status rules

Assign exactly one status per story and per use. The same person can be Consent on file
for a newsletter and Needs consent for a paid ad.

| Status | Conditions (all must hold for Consent on file) |
| --- | --- |
| Consent on file | Written or recorded consent; covers this use (for example fundraising, funder reporting, social media, press); covers this identification level (name, photo, age, location); not expired; not withdrawn; for minors, parent or guardian consent plus the young person's agreement plus the organization's review |
| Needs consent | Any condition above is missing or unclear: verbal only, "said it was fine", consent for a different use or channel, consent older than the organization's renewal period, minor without parent or guardian consent, story substantially changed since approval |
| Do not use | Withdrawn or declined; shared in a setting with an expectation of confidentiality (support group, counseling, crisis contact, private message to staff); risk of harm or identification cannot be reduced; person cannot be reached to confirm and the story is sensitive |

Withdrawal applies going forward: stop new uses at once, remove from owned channels
where practical, and record the withdrawal in the story bank. Printed materials already
distributed cannot be recalled; note that honestly to the person.

## What good consent covers

A consent form or recorded agreement should state:

- Which uses: newsletter, social media, website, appeals, funder reports, press, events,
  paid advertising.
- Identification: first name, full name, changed name, age, photo, video, voice,
  location, school.
- Duration and how to withdraw, and who to contact.
- That saying no does not affect services. This separation matters: participation in
  services must never depend on sharing a story.
- For minors: signatures or recorded agreement from a parent or guardian and the young
  person, in language the young person understands.

Consent is not a one-time event. Show the person the draft and the final version, and
re-ask when a story moves to a new use or audience.

## Minors

- Treat anyone under 18 at collection or at publication as a minor.
- Require parent or guardian consent and the young person's own agreement; either can
  say no. A young person's verbal "that's fine" is not enough.
- Require the organization's designated review (for example program lead and safety
  reviewer) before use.
- Use first name only or a changed name; no school, team, town, or identifying photo;
  avoid details that peers could recognize.
- Avoid stories about a minor's own mental-health crisis in fundraising materials.
- Revisit consent when the young person turns 18.

## De-identification checklist

Remove or change, unless consent explicitly covers it:

- Full name; use a changed name and note it to the reviewer, or a role ("a sophomore
  peer leader").
- School, campus, employer, small town, neighborhood, team.
- Exact dates, ages if rare in context, unique events ("the only student who...").
- Diagnoses, medications, health or disability details, immigration status, family
  circumstances.
- Photos, voice, handwriting, and metadata in images.

Check combinations: a role, a campus size, and a year can identify someone even when
no single detail does. When in doubt, generalize further or use a composite.

## Composites

- Build only from material whose consent allows composite or anonymous use.
- Label every appearance: "This is a composite story drawn from the experiences of
  several participants; names and details have been changed."
- No real photos, no real quotes attributed to the composite, no specific identifying
  details from any one person.
- Do not use a composite where a funder or reader would reasonably expect a real
  individual's account unless the label is prominent; follow the funder's rules.

## Privacy and records

- Do not paste participant records, case notes, student records, or health information
  into drafts. Work from the minimum needed.
- Flag material that may be covered by FERPA (student education records) or HIPAA
  (health information held by covered entities); the organization's counsel decides
  whether either applies.
- Never link a participant's story or service use to donor records.
- Store consent records with the story bank and keep them for the period the
  organization's policy sets.

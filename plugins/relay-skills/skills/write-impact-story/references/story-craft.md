# Story craft

## Strengths-based arc

| Beat | What it contains | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Person | Who they are and what they care about, in their words | Opening with a problem, label, or diagnosis |
| Context | The challenge as a situation or barrier (access, cost, stigma, distance) | Describing the person or community as broken or lacking |
| Action | What the person chose to do, with what the program offered | The organization or donor as the hero |
| Change | What the person says changed, within what the program measured | Clinical claims; claims of transformation, rescue, or cure |
| Next | What they are doing now or hope to do | A neat ending that implies the challenge is solved |

## Framing swaps (patterns)

| Problem type | Better approach |
| --- | --- |
| Deficit-first opening | Open with the person's goal, interest, or role |
| Before-and-after rescue arc | Show the person's own steps, with the program as one support among several |
| Pity or distress imagery | Images of people doing things, connecting, leading; with consent |
| Donor as savior ("your gift saved ...") | "Your gift supports [program description] so students can ..." |
| Clinical outcome language | Name the measured outcome: took part, learned, said they feel more confident, said they would reach out |
| Single-cause framing of hardship | Describe context and multiple factors |

## Participant voice and quotes

- Quote the person more than you narrate them.
- Trim quotes only for length; mark omissions with an ellipsis; never splice separate
  statements into one quote or change tense or meaning.
- Keep their words for their own experience, even when informal.
- Send every quote, including trimmed versions, for approval and record the status:
  Approved, Sent for approval, Changes requested, Declined.

## Formats

Word ranges are commonly used starting points; follow the organization's style and the
funder's limits exactly where they are set.

| Format | Length (starting point) | Shape | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short social post | 40 to 80 words | One moment, one quote, one line of context, call to action | Alt text for any image; changed name note if used |
| Newsletter feature | 250 to 500 words | Full arc with two or three quotes | Headline about the person's action, not their hardship |
| Grant or funder report vignette | 100 to 200 words, or the funder's limit | Person, program activity, measured change, link to the outcome the grant funded | Match the funder's required headings; no clinical claims; label composites |
| Speech excerpt | 60 to 150 words, about 30 to 60 seconds spoken | One scene, one quote, a pause line | Written for the ear: short sentences; consent must cover live events and recording |
| Appeal letter or email | 150 to 300 words for the story section | Person, action, invitation to support the program | No guilt, fear, or countdown urgency; no implication the person's wellbeing depends on the gift |

## Illustrative vignette (hypothetical, composite)

"This is a composite story drawn from several participants' experiences. A second-year
student we will call Sam joined a peer workshop because a friend had been having a hard
semester. Sam said, 'I learned how to start the conversation instead of waiting for the
right moment.' In the workshop's end-of-session survey, most participants said they felt
more confident reaching out to a friend. Sam now co-leads sessions in their residence
hall."

Note how the vignette names what was measured (self-reported confidence), uses a
labelled composite, makes Sam the actor, and makes no clinical claim. "Most
participants" must be replaced by the organization's actual measured figure with its
base before use.

## Final review before release

- Story bank status re-checked on the day of publication.
- Quote approval recorded for every quote.
- Program lead confirms accuracy; safety reviewer checks any crisis-related content.
- Minor stories: parent or guardian consent and organization review recorded.

# Agenda, consent agenda, dashboard and pre-reads

## Agenda design

Order by what needs the board's judgement, not by who reports:

| Order | Block | Share of time (guide) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call to order, quorum, conflict disclosures for this agenda | Brief | Chair asks for disclosures against the listed items |
| 2 | Consent agenda | Brief | One motion; any member may pull an item |
| 3 | Decisions requested | Largest share | Highest-stakes first while attention is fresh |
| 4 | Strategic discussion | Substantial | One or two questions the board can genuinely help with |
| 5 | Dashboard review | Short | Only exceptions and trends; the paper does the rest |
| 6 | Executive session | As needed | Without staff; separate minutes |
| 7 | Follow-up tracker review and adjournment | Brief | Confirm owners and dates |

The time shares are a guide to adjust; the principle is that decisions and strategy get most of the meeting.

## Consent agenda

Items that need approval but not discussion:

- minutes of the previous meeting;
- committee reports with no action requested;
- routine policy renewals with no changes;
- ratifying actions already authorized in principle (for example routine bank signatory updates, where the bylaws allow).

Rules: the consent agenda goes out with the packet; any member may ask for an item to be removed and taken separately, without needing a reason; items that involve a conflict of interest, a significant expense or a policy change do not go on the consent agenda.

## Decision papers

Each decision paper, one to two pages, with:

1. **The question** in one sentence, and "Purpose: decide".
2. **Why the board**: the bylaw, policy, funder or legal reason the board must decide.
3. **Background** in a few lines, with sources.
4. **Options**: at least two real options, including the status quo where relevant, each with costs, risks and consequences.
5. **Recommendation** and who makes it.
6. **Draft resolution.**
7. **Conflicts** known for this item.

## Board dashboard

Five to eight measures the board will act on. For each: definition, formula, source, period, target, trend. Examples with formulas (choose those that fit):

| Measure | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Months of operating reserve | unrestricted cash and investments available for operations ÷ (annual operating expenses excluding depreciation ÷ 12) | Define "available" consistently; exclude board-designated funds if the board wants a stricter view |
| Days cash on hand | unrestricted cash ÷ (annual operating expenses excluding depreciation ÷ 365) | Same basis each meeting |
| Year-to-date revenue versus budget | actual year-to-date revenue ÷ year-to-date budgeted revenue | Split restricted and unrestricted |
| Year-to-date expense versus budget | actual year-to-date expense ÷ year-to-date budgeted expense | Explain timing versus real variance |
| Funding pipeline, weighted | Σ (requested amount × probability) | Probabilities are Assumed unless based on the organization's own win history |
| Program reach | count of distinct participants or partner institutions served in the period | Define "served"; aggregate only |
| Measured program outcome | share of respondents reporting the measured change (for example knowledge or self-reported confidence), with response count | What was measured, never a treatment effect; suppress small groups |
| Donor retention | donors who gave in both periods ÷ donors in the prior period | From `plan-donor-stewardship` |

Small-group suppression: do not show any count or percentage for a group smaller than the threshold the organization sets. If none is set, show an assumed default (for example fewer than 10) and ask the organization to confirm. Suppress complementary cells too, so a hidden value cannot be recovered by subtraction.

## Pre-read standards

- Every paper states its purpose at the top: decide, discuss or note.
- A summary under one page, written for someone who reads only the summary.
- Numbers match the dashboard and financial statements; sources named.
- No participant-level data, student records or health information.
- Packet deadline set by the board (commonly several days before the meeting, so members can read); late papers become information items unless the chair decides otherwise.
- The packet includes the follow-up tracker and draft minutes of the last meeting.

# Resolutions, minutes and follow-up

## Draft resolutions

A resolution records exactly what the board decided. Write it so someone reading it years later knows what was authorized, by whom, and on what basis.

Structure:

- Optional background clauses ("Whereas ...") stating the facts the board relied on.
- Operative clauses ("Resolved, that ...") stating the action, amount, parties, limits and who is authorized to carry it out.
- For related-party or compensation decisions, a clause recording that conflicted members disclosed and did not participate, and the basis relied on (for example comparability data reviewed).

Illustrative, hypothetical:

> Resolved, that the Board approves the operating budget for the fiscal year beginning July 1 as presented in the finance committee's paper dated for this meeting, with total expenses of $1,200,000, and authorizes the Executive Director to manage expenditures within that budget, with any reallocation between major expense categories above an amount set by the finance policy requiring finance committee approval.

Counsel reviews resolutions that affect legal rights: bylaws amendments, contracts with related parties, borrowing, property, mergers, and anything a funder or regulator will rely on.

## Minutes

Minutes are the legal record of what the board did. They record actions, not a transcript of discussion.

Include:

- organization name, meeting type, date, time, place or format;
- members present and absent, others present, and that a quorum was present;
- conflicts disclosed and recusals, by item;
- each motion as adopted (or failed), the vote result, and names where the bylaws require or a member asks;
- the key basis for significant decisions, especially where the board relied on data, the auditor or counsel;
- reports received (by title, with papers attached or referenced);
- actions assigned, with owner and due date;
- time of adjournment; the signature of the officer who keeps the minutes, once approved.

Exclude: who said what, opinions, jokes, side conversations, and executive session content (keep executive session minutes separately, recording only actions taken).

Before-and-after illustration:

- Transcript style (avoid): "Member A said the budget was too tight; Member B disagreed and said revenue would improve; after a long discussion ..."
- Action style (use): "Following discussion of revenue assumptions, the board approved the budget as presented (motion carried; one abstention). Action: finance committee to present a revenue update at the next meeting (owner: treasurer)."

Hand writing up of minutes from notes to the board-meeting-minutes prompt; this skill supplies the template and rules.

## Follow-up tracker

One table carried from meeting to meeting:

| Action | Owner | Due | Status | Source meeting |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules:

- Every action in the minutes appears in the tracker.
- Status values are words: Not started, In progress, Done, Deferred (with reason).
- Items overdue by more than one meeting are raised by the chair, not silently carried forward.
- Done items stay visible for one meeting, then move to an archive.

## Questions for counsel

List each legal question as a question, with who will ask counsel and by when. Examples of questions that go to counsel rather than being answered in the meeting: whether an action complies with the bylaws; whether a related-party transaction is permitted; employment decisions about named staff; responses to data incidents; regulatory filings; contract terms; litigation. The packet notes when counsel's advice is pending.

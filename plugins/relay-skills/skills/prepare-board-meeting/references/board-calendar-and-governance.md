# Annual board calendar, duties and conflicts

## Annual board calendar

Anchor the calendar to the fiscal year end and to the dates the organization actually faces (audit fieldwork, Form 990 due date or extended due date, executive director review date). Illustrative placement for a fiscal year that ends June 30 and a board that meets quarterly; move items to match the organization's own cycle:

| Meeting | Governance items | Owner | Paper needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (after year end) | Officer elections if bylaws set them here; committee assignments; annual conflict of interest disclosures collected and reviewed; year-end unaudited results | Governance committee, treasurer | Disclosure summary; year-end results |
| Q2 | Audit or review results received with the auditor; Form 990 draft review before filing; executive director goals for the year confirmed | Audit or finance committee | Audit report and management letter; Form 990 draft |
| Q3 | Mid-year financial review; strategy session; policy reviews due this year | Finance committee; executive director | Budget versus actual; policy list |
| Q4 (before year start) | Next year's budget approval; executive director evaluation and compensation; board self-assessment; nominations for next year | Finance committee; executive or compensation committee; governance committee | Draft budget; evaluation summary and comparability data; self-assessment results |

Committee reports that need no action go on the consent agenda.

## Form 990 review

Form 990, the annual information return most tax-exempt organizations file with the IRS, asks in Part VI whether a copy of the return was provided to each voting member of the governing body before it was filed, and asks the organization to describe its review process. The form also asks about conflict of interest policies and how they are monitored, whistleblower and document retention policies, and the process for setting executive compensation. Plan the board or committee review before the filing date so the organization can answer those questions accurately. The accountant confirms what the organization must file and when.

## Audit

Whether the organization needs an audit, a review or neither can depend on state law, funder agreements and federal award rules, and thresholds change. The auditor and counsel confirm. Where there is an audit, the audit or finance committee should meet the auditor, including a portion without staff present, and the board should receive the report and management letter with management's responses.

## Executive director evaluation and compensation

- The board (or a committee it authorizes) evaluates the executive director against goals set at the start of the year.
- For compensation, US federal rules on excess benefit transactions provide a rebuttable presumption that compensation is reasonable when it is approved in advance by an authorized body whose members have no conflict of interest in the arrangement, the body relied on appropriate data about comparability, and it documented the basis for its decision at the time. Counsel or the accountant confirms how to meet these conditions.
- Anyone with a conflict (including the executive director and any family member on the board) does not take part in the discussion or vote.
- Deliberation happens in executive session; the resolution and the basis for the decision are recorded.

## Fiduciary duties

- **Care:** act in good faith with the care an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would use. In practice: attend, read the materials, ask questions, rely on information from people reasonably believed competent, and make informed decisions.
- **Loyalty:** put the organization's interest ahead of personal interest; disclose conflicts; do not use the position or confidential information for personal benefit; step back from conflicted decisions.
- **Obedience:** act consistently with the organization's mission, governing documents and applicable law. Some states and commentators treat it as part of care and loyalty rather than a separate duty.

State law defines the duties and any protections for directors who act in good faith on an informed basis. Counsel explains how they apply to a specific decision; the agent does not.

## Conflict check procedure

1. Keep a current disclosure for every board member (and key staff, if the policy covers them): employment, ownership, board seats, family relationships, and business dealings with the organization.
2. For each agenda decision, list the parties involved (vendors, partners, employees, funders) and compare against every disclosure.
3. For any match, classify under the policy: financial interest, family relationship, duality of interest (for example serving on another board that is a party).
4. Plan the handling the policy requires: disclosure to the board; the conflicted person answers factual questions if the policy allows, then leaves discussion and vote; the disinterested members decide whether the transaction is fair and in the organization's interest, considering alternatives.
5. Recount eligible voters and confirm quorum still holds for that item under the bylaws.
6. Record the disclosure, the recusal and the basis for the decision in the minutes.

Family relationships matter: a spouse or close relative of the executive director who sits on the board has a conflict on the executive director's evaluation and compensation.

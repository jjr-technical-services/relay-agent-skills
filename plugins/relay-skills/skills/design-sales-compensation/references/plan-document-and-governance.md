# Plan document and governance

## Plan document outline

A plan document should let a rep calculate their own pay without asking anyone. Typical sections:

1. **Purpose and plan period.** The business outcomes the plan supports; effective dates.
2. **Eligibility.** Roles covered, start and end of participation, leaves of absence, part-time treatment.
3. **Role-specific schedule.** OTE, base, target variable, measures and weights, quota, rate table, and a worked example of the pay calculation.
4. **Crediting rules.** Crediting event, credit amount, splits, overlays, territory transfers, multi-year and non-standard deals.
5. **Earning and payment.** When commission is earned, when it is paid, payment frequency, advances or draws if any, and treatment at termination.
6. **Adjustments.** Clawbacks, cancellations, returns, non-payment, errors and corrections.
7. **SPIFFs and special programmes.** How they are announced and governed.
8. **Large-deal and windfall review.** Thresholds and the process.
9. **Quota and territory changes.** When and how the company may change quotas or territories during the period, and how pay is affected.
10. **Disputes.** How to raise a dispute, who decides, and within what time.
11. **Plan administration.** The company's right to interpret the plan, the governing document if there is a conflict, and amendment procedure.
12. **Acknowledgement.** Signature and date, where required.

Legal wording for sections 5, 6, 9, 11, and 12 must come from counsel for each jurisdiction.

## Governance

- **Owner:** a named plan owner (often sales operations or a compensation committee with finance and HR).
- **Approval path:** sales leadership proposes, finance confirms cost, HR and counsel review, and the executive responsible signs off.
- **Exceptions:** written requests, a single approver with a backup, and an exceptions log reviewed at period end. Exceptions that recur signal a plan defect.
- **Calculation controls:** reconcile commission calculations with the system of record before payment; separate the person who calculates from the person who approves.
- **Review cadence:** review attainment distribution, cost of sales, and disputes at least once per period; redesign annually, not mid-period, unless the business changes materially.

## Rollout and communication

1. Brief managers first, with the worked examples and a list of likely questions.
2. Give each rep their own schedule with a personal worked example at 50, 100, and 150 percent.
3. Hold a question session; record answers and publish them to everyone so interpretations stay consistent.
4. Collect signed acknowledgements where required before the first payment.
5. Provide a way for reps to see their credited bookings and estimated earnings during the period.

## Legal and regulatory points to raise with counsel

Do not state the law for a jurisdiction; list the questions and involve counsel. Typical questions:

- Must the plan be in writing and signed, and what must it contain?
- At what point is a commission legally earned, and can the plan define that point?
- What are the deadlines for paying commissions, including after resignation or termination?
- Are deductions from wages, clawbacks, or offsets against future pay permitted, and under what conditions?
- Can the company change quotas, territories, or rates mid-period, and with what notice?
- Are there minimum-wage or overtime implications for reps with low base pay or non-exempt status?
- How do rules differ for reps working in other countries, including works council or consultation requirements?

## Signs a plan needs redesign

- Most reps miss quota year after year, or most exceed it by a wide margin.
- Cost of sales rises while bookings stay flat.
- Many disputes about credit, or a growing exceptions log.
- Reps cannot explain how they are paid.
- Behaviour the plan does not pay for (retention, multi-year terms, strategic products) is declining.

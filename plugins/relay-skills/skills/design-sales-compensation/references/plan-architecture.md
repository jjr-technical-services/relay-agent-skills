# Plan architecture: roles, pay mix, measures, crediting

## Role definitions

A plan pays roles, not people. Before touching numbers, write one line per role describing:

- **What it owns:** which customers or opportunities (new logos in a segment, the installed base, a product line, meetings booked for a team of account executives).
- **The motion:** hunter (acquires new customers), farmer (retains and grows existing customers), sales development representative or SDR (generates qualified meetings), overlay or specialist (supports another rep's deal with product or technical expertise), manager (responsible for a team's number).
- **Handoffs:** where the role's work starts and ends, so credit is unambiguous.

Two roles that both claim credit for the same event need an explicit split or overlay rule; otherwise the company pays twice or reps fight.

## Pay mix: why it differs by role

Pay mix follows one principle: **the more the individual rep's effort and skill determine whether the sale happens, the larger the variable share.** Other factors push the same way or the other way:

| Factor | Pushes toward more variable | Pushes toward more base |
|---|---|---|
| Rep's influence on the buying decision | High (rep creates and closes demand) | Low (customer would buy anyway, or a team closes) |
| Measurability of the result | Clear, attributable to one rep | Shared, delayed, or hard to attribute |
| Sales cycle length | Short to moderate | Very long, lumpy deals where a year can hinge on one deal |
| Non-selling duties | Few | Many (service, onboarding, admin) |

Applying the principle:

- **Hunters** usually carry the highest variable share, because winning a new customer depends heavily on the rep.
- **Farmers / account managers** usually carry less variable than hunters, because much of the renewal base would stay without individual heroics, and the role includes service work. Their variable pay should focus on growth (expansion) and retention of at-risk revenue, not on renewals that would happen anyway.
- **SDRs** usually carry a more base-heavy mix, because they generate opportunities rather than close them, and their measures (qualified meetings or pipeline created) are partly shared with the account executive.
- **Overlays and specialists** usually have a base-heavy mix and are paid on the team or product result they support.
- **Managers** are paid on the team's result, often with a mix close to their team's.

Commonly quoted mixes (for example, an even split for hunters and more base-heavy splits for farmers and SDRs) are heuristics that vary by company, market, and country. Present them only as a starting point, labelled as such, and prefer the user's licensed survey data and current plan.

## OTE levels

OTE comes from the labour market for the role, not from the plan math. Use the user's licensed pay surveys, recruiter data, or current offer data. If none is available, say so, and design with a placeholder OTE clearly labelled Assumed. Never invent a market figure.

## Measures

- **At most three per role.** Each additional measure dilutes attention. A primary measure should carry most of the variable pay.
- **Minimum weight.** A measure carrying only a small fraction of variable pay is usually ignored by reps. If a goal matters but cannot carry real weight, use a gate, a modifier, or a SPIFF instead of a separate measure.
- **Controllable and attributable.** The rep must be able to influence the measure, and the company must be able to credit it to the rep reliably from system data.
- **Aligned with company value.** Paying on bookings value when discounting is rampant invites discounting. Consider paying on ARR at list-price-adjusted value, adding a margin or discount modifier, or crediting multi-year contracts only on first-year value.

Measure types and when to use them:

| Measure | Use for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| New ARR or new bookings | Hunters | Discounting, front-loading multi-year terms |
| Net expansion ARR (expansion minus contraction) | Farmers | Churn hidden in other accounts; define the account set |
| Gross retention of at-risk ARR | Farmers | Must define "at-risk" in advance |
| Qualified meetings or sales-accepted opportunities | SDRs | Quality; require acceptance by the AE or a later-stage conversion |
| Pipeline created that converts within a window | SDRs | Delayed payment; explain timing clearly |
| Strategic product ARR | Any, as secondary measure | Weight must be meaningful or it is ignored |

## Linking measures to quota

Each measure needs its own quota (or a target for non-currency measures). Quotas should come from a capacity and potential model (see plan-sales-territories). Check the historical attainment distribution: if most reps historically miss, the plan will be experienced as a pay cut regardless of its rates.

## Crediting and split rules

Write down:

- **Crediting event:** signature, booking in the system of record, invoice, or cash receipt. Earlier events motivate faster; later events protect the company from paying on deals that never pay.
- **When commission is earned and when it is paid.** These can differ (earned on booking, paid in the month after invoice). Earned-versus-paid timing has legal consequences in many jurisdictions.
- **Credit amount:** full contract value, first-year value, ARR, or margin; treatment of discounts, free months, and professional services.
- **Splits:** a fixed rule for opportunities worked by two reps (territory transfers, cross-region deals). State whether each rep receives quota credit at 100 percent (double credit, which costs more) or shares one credit.
- **Overlays:** whether specialists receive credit in addition to the account owner (usually yes, at their own rate on their own quota).
- **Territory transfers and holdovers:** align with the holdover rules in the territory plan.
- **Leavers and joiners:** what happens to deals in flight when a rep leaves, and how a mid-period joiner's quota is prorated.
- **Disputes:** who decides and in what time.

# Stories, tone, attachments, and stewardship

## Consent gate for stories and quotes

A participant story or quote may appear in a funder report only when all of these are true:

1. Consent is recorded (written or in the organization's system), with the date, the person who consented, and the uses covered. A funder report and any onward sharing by the funder must be within scope.
2. For a minor: parental or guardian consent is recorded, the young person also agrees, and the organization's designated reviewer has approved the text.
3. The text is de-identified to the level agreed (first name only, a pseudonym, or no name), with details that could identify the person removed (school, exact age, small-town location, unusual circumstances).
4. The person, or for a minor the parent, has seen the final wording when the organization's policy requires it.

Status labels:

- **Consent on file**: all conditions met; state scope and date.
- **Needs consent**: any condition missing. Keep the story out of the report; do not paraphrase it or use it "anonymously" as a workaround. Replace it with aggregate data or a consented quote.

A verbal "that's fine" from a student is not recorded consent, and for a minor it is not sufficient on its own.

Story framing rules: strengths-based, in the person's own words where possible, focused on what they did and what changed for them in their terms. No before-and-after "rescue" arcs, no distress imagery, no clinical language. Hand drafting to write-impact-story.

## Tone candid about shortfalls

Program officers generally prefer early, plain disclosure to discovering a problem later. Patterns that work:

- Lead with the fact: "We reached 480 of the 600 students we planned to reach."
- Give the cause without blame or excess apology.
- Show what was learned and changed.
- Say what you are asking for, if anything (a target revision, a no-cost extension, a conversation).

Patterns to avoid: burying a miss after several paragraphs of positive news, vague phrases ("some challenges"), percentages without counts, superlatives, and promises the next period cannot keep.

Use a Status column with words (Ready, In progress, Not required), not checkboxes.

## Next-period plan

Activities and targets for the next period; any target changes flagged "Needs funder approval"; key risks with their mitigations; and dates of the next report.

## Stewardship follow-up

A report is also a relationship step. Plan one follow-up with an owner and date:

- a short call to walk the program officer through a shortfall and the fix;
- an invitation to observe a session (with participants' agreement and privacy protected);
- a thank-you note that names specifically what the funding made possible;
- for a final report, a conversation about what comes next, whether renewal or a planned close.

Record the follow-up in the CRM and move the funder's pipeline stage (research-grant-prospects) to Steward.

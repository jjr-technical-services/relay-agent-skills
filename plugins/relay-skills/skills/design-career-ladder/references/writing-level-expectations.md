# Writing level expectations that can be applied consistently

## Rules

1. **Observable.** Describe what someone does or produces that others could see: artifacts, decisions, outcomes, and how others experience working with them. Not traits ("is a strategic thinker") but behaviors ("identifies which of the team's problems matter most to the business and proposes the order to solve them").
2. **Differs in kind from adjacent levels.** Each step should change at least one of: what the person owns, the size of the audience or organization affected, the type of problem (defined for them versus defined by them), or how they make others effective. If the only difference is an adverb, rewrite.
3. **Outcome-based, not activity-based.** "Runs weekly one-to-ones" is an activity; "team members can describe their growth goals and how their work contributes to them" is an outcome.
4. **Cumulative by default.** State once that each level includes the expectations of the levels below. Then describe only what is new. This keeps each cell short.
5. **Neutral language.** Avoid words that are vague or known to invite biased judgments: "culture fit", "presence", "aggressive", "natural leader", "rockstar", "ninja". Replace each with the behavior intended.
6. **No experience proxies.** Years of experience, degrees, and certifications are not level expectations. They may appear in hiring guidance as typical, not required.
7. **Short.** One to three sentences per cell. Long cells get skimmed and cherry-picked.

## Degree words versus kind differences

| Degree words (avoid) | Kind difference (use) |
|---|---|
| "Strong communication skills" to "excellent communication skills" | "Explains decisions clearly to their own team" to "writes proposals that align several teams on a direction" |
| "Some autonomy" to "high autonomy" | "Breaks down assigned projects and flags risks early" to "identifies which projects the team should take on and secures agreement" |
| "Contributes to team goals" to "significantly contributes to team goals" | "Delivers their commitments toward the team's goals" to "sets the team's goals for their area with the manager" |
| "Mentors others" to "mentors many others" | "Helps newer teammates through code review and pairing" to "raises the practice of the whole team by setting standards others adopt" |

## Pattern for each cell

Use: **[verb] + [what] + [scope or audience] + [with what level of direction]**.

- "Designs and delivers features within the team's architecture, seeking review on significant design choices."
- "Defines the technical approach for a system used by several teams, and resolves disagreements about it."

## Illustrative expectations by dimension (engineering IC, levels 2 to 5)

These are examples of the writing pattern, not a recommended ladder. Replace with the organization's own calibrated wording.

**Complexity**
- IC2: Solves well-scoped problems where the approach is known; asks for help when stuck.
- IC3: Solves problems where the approach must be chosen among known options; identifies edge cases before they cause defects.
- IC4: Solves problems where the requirements themselves are unclear; turns an ambiguous goal into a plan others can execute.
- IC5: Identifies problems others have not seen, across teams, and frames them so the organization can decide whether to invest.

**Autonomy**
- IC2: Works from tasks defined by others; checks in frequently.
- IC3: Plans their own work on a feature; checks in at milestones.
- IC4: Sets direction for their area; informs the manager of decisions and escalates only cross-team conflicts.
- IC5: Sets direction across teams; seeks alignment with senior leaders on priorities.

**Leadership and influence**
- IC2: Gives useful feedback in reviews.
- IC3: Helps teammates grow through review and pairing; improves team practices when they see a gap.
- IC4: Leads projects with several contributors; mentors others toward the next level.
- IC5: Sets standards or practices adopted across several teams; develops future senior engineers.

## Manager track cells

Manager expectations should cover: team outcomes, hiring and team composition, developing people, performance management, operating the team (planning, prioritization, process), and cross-team leadership. The same rules apply. Example step in kind:

- M1: Manages one team; accountable for its delivery and for the growth of each team member.
- M2: Manages managers or several teams; accountable for the area's direction, structure, and the effectiveness of its managers.

## Common drafting problems and fixes

- **Too many dimensions**: cells become repetitive. Merge dimensions that never distinguish levels.
- **Levels described by title** ("acts like a senior"): circular. Describe the behavior.
- **Expectations only achievable with specific opportunities** ("led a major launch"): unfair to people without that assignment. Describe the capability demonstrated, with the launch as one example of evidence.
- **Hidden fourth track**: expectations at senior IC levels that are really management (hiring, performance reviews). Move them to the manager track or state them as optional contributions.

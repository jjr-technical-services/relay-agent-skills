# Crisis resources, content notes, and moderation

## Crisis resource rules

1. Use only the organization's approved crisis copy, verbatim. Represent it as the slot
   `ORGANIZATION-APPROVED CRISIS RESOURCE (insert approved wording)` until the approved copy is inserted by the
   organization.
2. Never invent a line, phone number, text code, chat link, or hours of service. Remove
   any resource in a draft that cannot be matched to the approved copy and list it in
   the output as removed pending verification.
3. General fact: in the US the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is reachable by call or text
   at 988. Where the organization has approved wording for it, use that wording.
4. Do not present the organization's own program or inbox as a crisis service unless it
   is one and the approved copy says so.
5. Outside the US, or for international audiences, ask the organization for approved
   copy for each country; do not guess.

## Placement by channel

| Channel | Placement rule |
| --- | --- |
| Instagram, Facebook, TikTok post | In the caption within the first lines, before "more"; also on the last carousel slide or end card. Not only in the first comment or link in bio |
| X or Threads | In the post itself or the first reply posted immediately by the account, and pinned where possible |
| LinkedIn | In the post body, not only in the comments |
| Blog or article | Short version under the content note at top, full version at the end |
| Email | Near the top for high-sensitivity emails and in the footer block |
| Event page and registration | On the page, in the confirmation email, and read aloud or shown at the event start and end |
| Video or podcast | On screen or spoken at start and end, and in the description |
| Press materials | At the end of the release, with a note inviting outlets to include it |

## Content notes

Use when the piece discusses suicide, self-harm, or a death in any detail, or tells a
personal story involving a crisis. Keep it short, factual, and paired with the slot:

"Content note: this story discusses suicide. If you are having a hard time, support is
available: `ORGANIZATION-APPROVED CRISIS RESOURCE (insert approved wording)`."

Do not use a content note as permission to include unsafe detail; the checklist still
applies.

## Images and headlines

- No photographs of the location of a death, memorial sites tied to a location, method
  cues, notes, or grieving families in distress.
- Prefer images of connection and support, the person's life (with family permission),
  or neutral graphics.
- Headlines and preview text must be safe out of context, since many readers see only
  those. No method, cause, or sensational verb; no teaser built on the word "suicide".

## Comment moderation plan

Write this for every social post on a high-sensitivity topic. Fill in names and times
from the organization; label defaults as assumptions.

| Element | What to specify |
| --- | --- |
| Owner and backup | Named people, not "the team" |
| Watch window | Close watch in the first hours after posting and daily checks for the following days; set exact times with the organization (default shown as an assumption) |
| Pre-written replies | Thank-you reply; reply pointing to the crisis slot; reply to misinformation; reply to someone grieving |
| Hide or remove | Method or location detail, blaming the person or family, graphic content, harassment, spam, unapproved crisis numbers posted by others |
| Someone may be in crisis | Reply promptly with the approved crisis copy, send a private message with the same copy if the platform allows, follow the organization's escalation procedure, and notify the safety reviewer; staff do not counsel or assess |
| Turn comments off | When volume exceeds moderation capacity, when harmful content recurs, or when the family requests it |
| Records | Keep a log of removed comments and crisis replies per the organization's policy |

## When to route to the clinical or safety reviewer

- Any high-sensitivity content before publishing.
- Any content aimed at youth or likely to reach minors.
- Any named person who died, or a lived-experience story.
- Any statement about symptoms, warning signs, treatment, or outcomes.
- Any paid promotion of high-sensitivity content.
- Any comment thread where someone may be in crisis (after following the escalation
  procedure).

# Language guide

People-first and non-stigmatizing language. When the organization supplies its own
phrasing for its audiences, use that first. These are commonly used swaps; they are
not a complete style guide.

## Suicide and self-harm

| Avoid | Use instead | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The crime verb with "suicide" | Died by suicide; death by suicide | Suicide is not a crime; the old verb carries blame |
| Successful or failed attempt; completed suicide | Died by suicide; survived a suicide attempt | Success language frames death as an achievement |
| Suicide victim | Person who died by suicide | Keeps the focus on the person |
| Suicidal person (as identity) | Person having thoughts of suicide | People-first |
| Epidemic, skyrocketing, crisis of (as a hook) | State the figure with source and period | Avoids shock framing and normalizing |
| Cutter | Person who self-harms; person with lived experience of self-harm | People-first |

## Mental health and illness

| Avoid | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| Mentally ill person; the mentally ill | Person with a mental health condition; people living with ... |
| Suffers from, is a victim of, battling | Has, lives with, is experiencing |
| Crazy, insane, psycho, nuts (any use, including casual) | Specific description, or remove |
| Addict, abuser | Person with a substance use disorder; person who uses drugs |
| Normal versus mentally ill | People with and without a diagnosis |
| Clinical terms used casually (OCD, bipolar, depressed as mood words) | Plain description of the feeling |
| At-risk youth (as a label for a whole group) | Young people facing [named barriers]; follow the organization's phrasing |
| Broken, damaged, hopeless (describing people) | Describe the situation and the strengths present |

## Communities and deficit framing

- Describe barriers and systems, not deficits in people: "students with limited access
  to campus counseling" rather than "underserved students who lack coping skills".
- Name groups the way they name themselves; follow the organization's guide.
- Avoid implying that a community, identity, or family type causes poor mental health.

## Help-seeking language

- Normalize: "Reaching out is a sign of strength" is a cliché to some audiences; prefer
  concrete action: "If you are going through a hard time, you can talk to someone now:"
  followed by the crisis slot.
- Give friends a role: how to notice, ask directly and kindly, listen, and connect to
  help.
- Avoid promising outcomes ("you will feel better"); use "support is available" and
  "many people find that talking helps".

## Program and outcome claims

Describe what was measured: participation, knowledge gained, self-reported confidence,
help-seeking intentions. Never write that a program, post, or donation treats, cures,
prevents suicide, or saves lives.

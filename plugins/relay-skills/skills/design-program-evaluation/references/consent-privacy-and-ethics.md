# Consent, privacy, and ethics

These are planning prompts, not legal advice. The organization's counsel and partner institutions decide what rules apply.

## Consent and assent

- **Adults:** informed consent covering purpose, what is asked, time needed, voluntariness, how data is used and stored, who sees results, and whom to contact.
- **Minors (under 18 in most US states):** permission from a parent or guardian, plus the young person's own assent (their agreement, in age-appropriate language). A young person's refusal should be respected even when a parent has given permission.
- **Active versus passive permission:** active permission requires a signed or recorded yes; passive (opt-out) permission assumes yes unless a parent declines. Schools, districts, and some laws require active permission for certain topics. Check the partner's policy and ask counsel.
- **In school settings:** the Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment (PPRA) covers certain surveys in programs funded by the US Department of Education and lists protected areas that include mental or psychological problems of the student or family; such surveys can require prior written parental consent. Whether PPRA applies to a particular survey is for counsel and the district to decide.
- **Online collection from children under 13:** the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) may apply to online tools collecting personal information from children under 13. Flag for counsel.
- **Voluntary participation:** state that program participation does not depend on survey participation. Offer an alternative activity during survey time.

## Research versus evaluation

Program evaluation for internal improvement and accountability is often not treated as research requiring IRB review. If results will be published or presented as generalizable knowledge, or a university partner is involved, the activity may need IRB review. Decide before collecting data; retroactive review is hard.

## Sensitive topics and safety

- Do not ask about self-harm, suicidal thoughts, or abuse unless a clinical partner and written response protocol are in place.
- Put the organization-approved crisis resource on every survey and session material that touches mental-health topics, using the organization's wording verbatim. In the US, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is reachable by call or text at 988; use the organization's approved copy where one exists.
- Know mandated-reporting obligations of staff under state law and partner policy; open-ended items can surface disclosures. Plan who reviews open-ended responses and how quickly.

## Privacy

- **Minimize:** collect only what answers an evaluation question. Avoid names; use codes.
- **Separate:** store any linking key apart from responses, with restricted access.
- **Retain:** set a retention period and a deletion step.
- **Share:** partner data-sharing agreements should say what is shared, in what form (aggregate only is safest), and for how long.
- **Student records:** data from education records may be covered by FERPA, which governs disclosure of education records by schools that receive US Department of Education funds. Health information held by covered entities and their business associates may be covered by HIPAA. Most education-focused nonprofits are not HIPAA covered entities, but applicability is for counsel to decide.

## Small-cell suppression

- Set a threshold N: no reported cell (count or percentage) is based on fewer than N respondents. The organization chooses N; 10 is a common default and 5 is also used. Label the chosen value as confirmed or assumed.
- Apply it to every breakdown, including by site, grade, or identity group. A site with fewer participants than N is reported only in the combined total.
- **Complementary suppression:** if one cell in a row is suppressed and the row total is shown, suppress at least one more cell so the hidden value cannot be calculated by subtraction.
- Watch for 0% or 100% results in small groups: they reveal every member's answer.
- Never place participant-level data, quotes that could identify someone, or health information in reports.

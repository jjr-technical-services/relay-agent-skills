# Governance, cadence, and the budget-owner template

## Planning calendar

Work backwards from the approval date. A typical sequence (durations depend on company size; set them with the user):

1. Leadership sets targets and planning assumptions; finance issues guidance and templates.
2. Budget owners build baseline and requests; finance supports driver modelling.
3. Finance consolidates, sizes the gap, and runs first scenarios.
4. Leadership reconciliation sessions rank and decide asks.
5. Final model, scenarios, and cash plan prepared; review by the chief executive and finance lead.
6. Board or owner approval.
7. Plan loaded into the reporting system at department × account × month; owners receive their final budgets.

## Approval and accountability

- Each department budget has one named owner who is accountable for spend against it.
- Define spending authority: which amounts an owner can commit alone, which need finance or executive approval, and how unbudgeted spend is approved.
- Define what counts as a change: moving money between accounts within a department, between departments, or adding headcount. Headcount additions usually need explicit approval even when the money exists.
- Record the approved plan as a fixed baseline. Later forecasts do not overwrite it; they are compared against it.

## Reforecast cadence

- **Monthly**: actuals versus budget and versus latest forecast by department; owners explain material variances (hand off to budget-variance-explainer).
- **Quarterly**: full reforecast of the remaining months from updated drivers (bookings, hiring actuals, churn). Keep the original budget for accountability and the forecast for decisions.
- **Rolling forecast**: some companies extend the forecast horizon each quarter so there are always four to six quarters ahead; it reduces the annual cycle's cliff but needs driver discipline.
- **Trigger-based**: when a scenario trigger fires, run the pre-agreed action and reforecast.

Materiality thresholds for variance explanations (an amount and a percentage) should be set with the user; there is no universal threshold.

## Budget-owner template

Give each owner the same structure. Finance pre-fills the baseline; the owner completes the rest.

**Section A — Baseline (pre-filled by finance, confirmed by owner)**

| Line | Type (contractual / variable / fixed / discretionary) | Current annual run rate | Contract end or renewal month | Owner comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Section B — Headcount**

| Name or role | Status (existing / open req / new request) | Level | Location | Start month | Base salary | Variable pay | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Section C — Incremental requests**

| Request | Amount | Months affected | Driver or outcome it moves | Expected effect and timing | Evidence | Consequence if not funded | Priority (1 = highest) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Section D — Savings and stops**

| Item | Annual saving | Effective month | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|

**Section E — Assumptions used**

List every driver value the owner relied on, with its source. Owners must use company-wide planning assumptions (pay increases, burden rates, currency) as issued.

**Section F — Risks and dependencies**

What the department's plan depends on from other departments, and what would cause it to overspend.

## Packaging for approval

- One-page summary: targets versus plan, key assumptions, headcount, cash and runway, and the main risks.
- Scenario table with triggers and actions.
- Reconciliation log showing what was not funded and why.
- Appendix: department budgets, headcount plan, monthly P&L, and cash bridge.

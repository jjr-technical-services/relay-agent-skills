# Utilization, overlaps, and savings levers

## Utilization

Define terms per tool and state them:

- **Purchased seats**: seats paid for under the contract.
- **Assigned seats**: seats allocated to named users.
- **Active seats**: users with qualifying activity in a stated window. "Qualifying activity" differs by tool: a sign-in may be enough for a viewer tool, while a design or analytics tool may need a created or edited item. Use the most meaningful signal the admin console provides and say which.
- **Window**: commonly 30 or 90 days. Use 90 days (or longer) for tools with periodic use (quarterly reporting, annual reviews) to avoid cutting seats people genuinely need.

Metrics:

```
Assignment rate = assigned ÷ purchased
Utilization     = active ÷ purchased
Cost per active user (annual) = annual cost ÷ active users
Leaver seats    = seats assigned to people no longer employed (join to HR roster)
```

Leaver seats are the cleanest saving: no user loses anything. Recover them first and fix offboarding so they do not recur.

Different seat types (full, viewer, guest, admin) are priced differently. Utilization should be measured per seat type; a common saving is moving light users to a cheaper seat type.

## Overlaps and duplicates

Within each category, list the tools and ask:

- **Duplicate**: the same product purchased more than once (separate team accounts, separate cards). Consolidating onto one account usually saves money through volume pricing and simplifies administration, with little change for users.
- **Functional overlap**: different products doing the same job (two video meeting tools, three project trackers). Consolidation saves more but disrupts work; it needs a migration plan.
- **Partial overlap**: a platform the company already pays for includes features that replace a point tool. Check that the included feature actually meets the need before claiming the saving.

Overlap tests for each candidate pair:

1. Do the user groups overlap, or does each team use only one?
2. What is unique about each tool, and is that uniqueness needed? Ask the heaviest users.
3. What integrations, automations, and data depend on each?
4. What is the contract position of each (which renews first, which has more committed seats)?
5. What does migration cost in time, training, and parallel running?

Decision rule: consolidate onto the tool that meets the must-have needs of the largest active user group at the lowest total cost including migration, and time the switch to the renewal of the tool being retired.

## Savings levers and arithmetic

Label every figure as an **estimate** and state the formula. Use contract prices, not list prices.

| Lever | When it applies | Estimated annual saving |
|---|---|---|
| Recover leaver seats | Seats assigned to departed staff | leaver seats × price per seat × 12 (if monthly) or at renewal if committed |
| Right-size seats | Purchased well above active use | (purchased − target seats) × annual price per seat, where target = active + agreed buffer |
| Downgrade seat type or tier | Users or the company do not use premium features | users moved × (current price − lower price) × 12, or tier price difference |
| Consolidate | Duplicate or overlapping tools | cost of retired tool − added cost on the retained tool − one-time migration cost (show one-time separately) |
| Renegotiate at renewal | Large contracts, multi-year options, volume growth | Unknown until negotiated; record the target and the basis, not a promised saving |
| Cancel | No active users, or need met elsewhere | full annual cost from the earliest exit date, less any exit cost |
| Change billing | Monthly billing for stable, long-used tools | difference between monthly and annual pricing, weighed against lost flexibility |

The buffer above active users is a judgement: account for planned hiring, seasonal users, and the provider's minimum seat counts. State it.

### Timing: when a saving is real

Most contracts commit seats or tiers for the term. A seat reduction recorded mid-term often saves nothing until renewal. For each lever state the **earliest realization date**: immediately (monthly plans with no minimum), at next renewal, or after an exit fee. Annual saving in the current fiscal year = monthly saving × months remaining after the realization date.

### Stages of savings

Track every item through:

- **Identified**: the analysis shows a saving is available (estimate).
- **Committed**: the owner has agreed and the change is scheduled or notice is given.
- **Realized**: the invoice or card charge has actually dropped.

Report the three separately. Only realized savings should reduce the budget; identified savings are a pipeline.

## Negotiation handoff

For contracts where renegotiation is the lever, prepare: current price and seat history, utilization evidence, the renewal and notice dates, alternatives considered, and the target outcome. The negotiation itself belongs in negotiate-tactically; comparing alternative vendors in vendor-comparison.

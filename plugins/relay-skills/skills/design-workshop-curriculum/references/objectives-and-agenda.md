# Objectives and agenda

## Learning objectives

Bloom's revised taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001) orders cognitive processes from remember to create. Choose the level the session can realistically reach; a 60-minute session usually reaches remember, understand, and apply.

| Level | Useful verbs | Example objective (fictional peer scenario) |
|---|---|---|
| Remember | list, name, identify, recall | Participants will name three campus support options. |
| Understand | describe, explain, summarize, distinguish | Participants will describe common signs that a peer may be struggling. |
| Apply | demonstrate, use, practice, role-play | Participants will demonstrate, in a practice scenario, how to open a supportive conversation. |
| Analyze | compare, differentiate, examine | Participants will distinguish situations that need an immediate referral from ones that call for a check-in. |
| Evaluate | judge, choose, justify | Participants will choose an appropriate next step for a scenario and justify it. |
| Create | plan, design, draft | Participants will draft a personal plan for staying connected to support. |

ABCD form: Audience (who), Behavior (the observable verb), Condition (under what circumstances), Degree (how well). Example: "By the end of the session (condition), resident assistants (audience) will identify (behavior) at least two referral options for a scenario (degree)."

Avoid "know", "learn", "understand" as the behavior (use "explain" or "describe"), "appreciate", "be aware of", and "feel". Confidence is measured in the survey, not written as an objective.

## Agenda principles

- **Open with safety and purpose:** welcome, why we are here, content note, how to step away, norms (confidentiality within the room, speak from general experience, no one has to share).
- **Chunk input:** 10 to 15 minutes of input before a practice, discussion, or poll.
- **Practice with fictional scenarios:** never ask participants to share their own mental-health history.
- **Leave time for the resource walk-through:** show how to reach each resource, not just a list.
- **Measure before the close:** collect the post measure while people are still present.
- **Close with grounding:** a brief, optional reflection or a takeaway, not a sudden stop after heavy content.
- **Buffer:** plan what to cut if running late (usually part of a debrief or a second scenario), never the resource slide or close.

## Patterns by length

Durations are illustrative starting points; always recompute the sum.

45 minutes: welcome and content note 4 + input 10 + practice 12 + debrief 6 + resources 6 + measure 4 + close 3 = 45.

60 minutes: welcome and content note 5 + objectives and norms 3 + input 12 + practice 15 + debrief 8 + resources 7 + measure 5 + close 5 = 60.

90 minutes: welcome and content note 6 + norms 4 + input 15 + practice 15 + debrief 8 + break 5 + input 10 + practice 12 + resources 7 + measure 4 + close 4 = 90.

## Activity formats

| Format | Virtual | In person | Opt-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scenario practice in pairs or trios | Breakout rooms with the scenario pasted in chat and on a slide; a facilitator visits rooms | Printed scenario cards | Observe, or take notes for the group |
| Poll or knowledge check | Platform poll, anonymous | Hands, cards, or a phone poll | Skip the question |
| Chat waterfall (everyone types, sends together) | Chat | Sticky notes | Do not post |
| Resource scavenger hunt | Participants find a resource page and share one fact | Handout with links or QR codes | Follow along |
| Reflection | Private writing, not shared | Private writing | Always optional |

Instructions should state the task, grouping, time, what to bring back, and how to get help, in that order.

# Accessibility and adaptation

## Accessibility plan

| Area | Practice |
|---|---|
| Captions | Turn on live captions for every virtual session; for larger, public, or recorded sessions, arrange human captioning (CART) where the budget allows. Check the platform setting before the session. |
| Materials in advance | Send slides and handouts a few days before (48 hours is a common courtesy) so people using screen readers, translation, or preparation time can review. |
| Plain language | Short sentences, common words, jargon defined on first use. Aim for material a general audience reads easily. |
| Slides | Large text, high contrast, one idea per slide, no information carried by color alone, alt text for images, described charts. |
| Speaking | Describe what is on screen; read poll questions and chat highlights aloud; one speaker at a time. |
| Participation | Offer chat, voice, anonymous polls, and private reflection; cameras optional. |
| Breaks | Include a break in sessions longer than 60 minutes. |
| Language access | Materials and, where needed, interpretation in the audience's languages. Translation of mental-health content should be reviewed by a fluent reviewer. |
| Accommodations | Ask in the registration form; name a contact for accommodation needs. |
| In person | Step-free access, seating options, quiet space to step away, microphones used every time. |

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are the common reference for digital materials; the organization may set a specific level.

## Adaptation guide

Keep fixed across all audiences: the content note, opt-out, safe-messaging practice, no screening, the resource slide with the organization-approved crisis resource, and the non-clinical scope.

| Element | Students | Families | Staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objectives | Recognize signs in peers, start a conversation, connect a friend to support | Talk with their young person, know where to turn, support their own wellbeing | Recognize signs, respond within their role, refer using institutional procedure |
| Examples | Peer scenarios in residence halls, classes, group chats | Home conversations, school communication | Office hours, advising, supervision |
| Tone and language | Direct, peer-level, avoid lecturing | Warm, non-blaming, acknowledge caregiver stress | Role-focused, procedure-aware |
| Length | 45 to 60 minutes | 60 minutes, evening or weekend options | 60 to 90 minutes |
| Resources | Campus and student resources plus the crisis resource | Family and community resources plus the crisis resource | Institutional referral procedure, employee assistance program, plus the crisis resource |
| Sensitivities | Peers may be close to someone struggling; mixed groups of friends | Parents may be worried about a specific child; offer private follow-up | Supervisors and reports in the same room may limit openness |
| Minors | Parental consent and host approval for under-18 participants and any surveys | Children not present unless designed for it | Not applicable |
| Measure | Knowledge and confidence to help a peer | Confidence to start a conversation; knowledge of resources | Confidence to respond and refer; knowledge of procedure |

# Design Workshop Curriculum

Design a session that a facilitator who did not write it can deliver well, that participants with different access needs can fully join, that treats a sensitive topic safely, and whose results can be measured. The deliverable is a complete, reusable package: objectives, timed agenda, activities, facilitator guide, materials, measure, follow-up, and adaptations.

Workshops on mental health, suicide prevention, grief, or crisis topics are education, not therapy. They build knowledge, skills, confidence, and connection to resources. They never screen, assess, diagnose, or counsel participants.

## Inputs to gather

Ask for or retrieve these; proceed with labeled placeholders ("Assumed:") where missing.

- **Client and audience:** the institution, who attends (students, families, staff, a mixed group), approximate number, age range (flag minors), prior knowledge, languages, and relationship to each other (peers, supervisors and staff together).
- **The need:** what prompted the request, what the client hopes participants will do differently, and any recent events affecting the group (a recent death in the community changes the design; see the reference on facilitation).
- **Format and constraints:** virtual or in-person, total minutes, platform, room setup, recording plans, class size.
- **Existing approved content:** the organization's curricula, slides, approved language, and its approved crisis resource copy. Use approved content verbatim where it exists.
- **Facilitators:** who delivers, their training, whether a second facilitator or support person is available.
- **Accessibility needs** known in advance and the client's captioning capacity.
- **Measurement and follow-up expectations** from the client or funder.

## Workflow

1. **Analyze audience and need.** Write two or three sentences on who the participants are, what they already know, what they need to be able to do, and what could make the topic hard for them personally. Separate what the client asked for from what the audience needs; note conflicts.
2. **Write measurable learning objectives.** Three or four at most for a 60-minute session. Use the ABCD form (Audience, Behavior, Condition, Degree) where useful and a verb from Bloom's revised taxonomy (remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create). Avoid unmeasurable verbs such as "know", "learn", "appreciate", or "be aware of". Read `references/objectives-and-agenda.md`.
3. **Check the request against safety practice.** Read `references/safe-facilitation.md` for any mental-health topic. Common conflicts to resolve and explain to the client: live screening or self-assessment quizzes about symptoms (remove; replace with a knowledge check or a reflection on support options), survivor or lived-experience stories that include method or location detail (keep the story only if the speaker is prepared and the content follows safe-messaging practice), and crisis numbers typed from memory (replace with the organization-approved crisis resource). List each change under "Changes to the request".
4. **Build the agenda to the minute.** Open with welcome, purpose, content note, and how to step away. Alternate input with practice at least every 10 to 15 minutes. Close with the resource slide, the post measure, and a grounding close. Add start times and durations; durations must add to the session length exactly.
5. **Design activities for the format.** For each activity: purpose (which objective), instructions as the facilitator will say them, virtual version, in-person version, time, grouping, and the opt-out option. Practice activities use scenarios about a fictional peer, never participants' own experiences.
6. **Plan accessibility.** Captions (live captions or human captioning for larger or public sessions), plain language, slides and handouts sent in advance, readable slides, described visuals, multiple ways to participate (chat, voice, anonymous poll), breaks for sessions over 60 minutes, and materials in the audience's languages where needed. Read `references/accessibility-and-adaptation.md`.
7. **Write the facilitator guide.** Segment by segment: goal, what to say (key points, not a script to read), what to do, timing cues, what to cut if running late, and how to respond to hard moments (a disclosure, a distressed participant, a question beyond scope, a stigmatizing comment).
8. **Add the pre/post measure.** Three to five items tied to the objectives: knowledge (one or two items with a correct answer), self-reported confidence, and intention. A retrospective pre-post item ("before this session / now") works well in a single sitting. Non-clinical items only. Hand analysis to `analyze-survey-results`.
9. **Draft the follow-up.** A short email outline sent within a few days: thanks, the key takeaways, the resource list including the organization-approved crisis resource, the slides, the feedback link if not completed, and one next step. The workshop-follow-up-email prompt can polish the final text.
10. **Write the adaptation guide** for students, families, and staff: what changes in objectives, examples, language, length, and resources for each, and what stays fixed (safety elements).

## Output

### Output contract

Use these section headings verbatim, as Markdown headings, in this order:

1. Audience and needs
2. Changes to the request
3. Learning objectives
4. Agenda
5. Timing check
6. Content note
7. Activities
8. Facilitator guide
9. Resource slide
10. Accessibility plan
11. Pre/post measure
12. Follow-up email and resources
13. Adaptation guide

Use these exact labels where they apply: None, Responding to hard moments, ORGANIZATION-APPROVED CRISIS RESOURCE, Students, Families, Staff.

Do not rename, merge or skip a heading; if a section has nothing to report, keep the heading and say so in one line. Do not put square brackets or curly braces in headings or labels.

### Section contents

Do not use Markdown checkboxes.

1. **Audience and needs** - who, what they know, what they need, sensitivities.
2. **Changes to the request** - each requested element changed, why, and what replaces it. Write "None" if nothing changed.
3. **Learning objectives** - numbered, each with its Bloom's verb in bold.
4. **Agenda** - table: `Start | Minutes | Segment | Method | Objective | Materials`.
5. **Timing check** - the sum of the Minutes column shown as an addition equal to the session length, plus what to cut first if running late.
6. **Content note** - the exact wording read and shown at the start, including how to step away and where to find support.
7. **Activities** - for each: purpose, instructions, virtual version, in-person version, opt-out.
8. **Facilitator guide** - by segment, plus "Responding to hard moments".
9. **Resource slide** - the slide text, with the crisis line written as the slot ORGANIZATION-APPROVED CRISIS RESOURCE (insert approved wording) unless the organization's approved copy is supplied, plus local and campus resources as slots in the same capitalized style for the client to fill. Write slots in capitals without square or curly brackets so they stand out and survive copying into other tools.
10. **Accessibility plan** - captions, materials in advance, plain language, participation options, breaks.
11. **Pre/post measure** - the items and response scales.
12. **Follow-up email and resources** - outline and resource list.
13. **Adaptation guide** - table: `Element | Students | Families | Staff`.

### Illustrative timing (hypothetical 60-minute virtual session)

Welcome and content note 5 + objectives and norms 3 + knowledge input 12 + scenario practice in breakouts 15 + debrief 8 + resources walk-through 7 + post measure 5 + close 5 = 60. The Timing check shows this addition explicitly.

## Sample agenda (hypothetical 60-minute virtual session for peer supporters)

| Start | Minutes | Segment | Method | Objective | Materials |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 5 | Welcome, purpose, content note, how to step away | Facilitator, slide | Safety | Content note slide |
| 0:05 | 3 | Objectives and group norms | Facilitator | Safety | Norms slide |
| 0:08 | 12 | Signs a peer may be struggling; what support looks like | Short input, poll | 1 | Slides, poll |
| 0:20 | 15 | Practice opening a conversation with a fictional peer | Breakout trios | 2 | Scenario cards in chat |
| 0:35 | 8 | Debrief: what worked, what was hard | Whole group, chat | 2 | None |
| 0:43 | 7 | Walk through each resource and how to reach it | Facilitator, screen share | 3 | Resource slide |
| 0:50 | 5 | Post measure | Anonymous form | Measure | Form link |
| 0:55 | 5 | Takeaway and grounding close | Optional chat | Close | Resource slide stays on screen |

Timing check for this sample: 5 + 3 + 12 + 15 + 8 + 7 + 5 + 5 = 60. If running late, shorten the debrief to 4 minutes; never cut the resource walk-through or the close.

## Sample pre/post measure (hypothetical items)

Use a retrospective pre-post format in a single session: participants rate each confidence item twice, "Before this session" and "Now", on the same scale.

| Item | Type | Response options |
|---|---|---|
| Which of these is a campus support option described today? | Knowledge | Multiple choice, one correct answer |
| Name one sign that a peer may be struggling. | Knowledge | Short open answer |
| How confident are you that you could start a conversation with a peer who seems to be struggling? | Confidence (before / now) | Not at all, Slightly, Moderately, Very, Extremely confident |
| How confident are you that you could help a peer find support? | Confidence (before / now) | Same scale |
| How likely are you to share a resource from today with someone in the next month? | Intention | Very unlikely to Very likely, plus Prefer not to say |

Keep items anonymous unless the client needs linking; never ask about participants' own mental health. Put the resource slide text at the end of the form.

## Handling client requests that conflict with safety practice

Explain changes to the client in one or two plain sentences each, framed around the shared goal:

| Request | Change | How to explain it |
|---|---|---|
| Live symptom screening or self-assessment quiz | Replace with a knowledge check about signs and resources | Screening belongs in clinical settings with follow-up; a knowledge check measures what the session teaches |
| Lived-experience story with method detail | Keep the story focused on getting help, with the speaker prepared and supported, or use an approved written account | Method detail can increase risk for people who are struggling; stories of help-seeking and recovery are protective |
| Crisis number typed into the slides by the client or facilitator | Use the organization-approved crisis resource verbatim | Approved wording is checked and kept current |
| No time for opt-out or content note | Keep both; trim input instead | Both take under two minutes and let people stay |
| Recording the session with participant discussion | Record only the presentation portions, with notice | Protects participants who share in discussion |

## Quality checks

- Objectives use measurable Bloom's verbs, and each agenda segment serves an objective or a named safety or logistics purpose.
- Minutes add exactly to the session length; start times are consistent.
- Practice alternates with input at least every 15 minutes.
- A content note, an opt-out, and the resource slide are present for any mental-health topic; the resource slide appears on screen and in the follow-up.
- No crisis line, number, or text code is invented. Use the organization's approved copy verbatim or the slot. In the US, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is reachable by call or text at 988; use the organization's wording where one exists.
- No screening, self-assessment of symptoms, diagnosis, or counseling in the session; measure items are non-clinical.
- Any story follows safe-messaging practice: no method or location detail, no framing of suicide as a solution or as caused by a single event, hope and help-seeking included. Say "died by suicide" or "attempted suicide", never "committed suicide".
- Accessibility plan covers captions, advance materials, and more than one way to participate.
- The adaptation guide keeps safety elements fixed across audiences.

## Boundaries

- Facilitators are educators, not clinicians. Disclosures and participants in distress are handled under the organization's and the host institution's procedures; the guide points to them rather than improvising.
- Mandated-reporting duties depend on state law and the host institution; confirm with the organization.
- The client institution approves content for its audience, and the organization's leadership approves any new or changed curriculum before delivery.
- Pricing, contracts, and scheduling belong to the workshop inquiry and invoicing processes, not this design.

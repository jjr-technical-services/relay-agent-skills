# Forecast categories and deal inspection

## Forecast categories

Categories express the owner's judgement about whether a deal will close in the period. They are separate from stage, which describes where the buyer is in their process. Write inclusion criteria down and apply them the same way to every rep. The criteria below are a starting template; adapt them to the team's sales process and keep the adapted version with the forecast.

| Category | Meaning | Inclusion criteria (all must be true unless noted) |
|---|---|---|
| Closed | Signed and booked | Signed order in the system of record |
| Commit | Will close in the period; the owner would stake their credibility on it | Economic buyer engaged and has confirmed intent to buy in the period; decision criteria met (technical validation or equivalent complete); paper process mapped with named people and dates, and the remaining steps fit before period end; pricing agreed or within approved discount; a dated next step with the buyer; no unresolved competitive evaluation |
| Best case | Could close in the period if identified events happen | Qualified opportunity with a buyer-confirmed timeline in the period; a specific, named event or approval stands between it and commit; the owner can say what must happen and by when |
| Pipeline | Qualified but unlikely to close in the period, or too early to judge | Qualified opportunity; close date in the period but commit and best-case criteria not met, or close date in a future period |
| Omitted | Excluded from the forecast | Close date in the past, no activity with the buyer within the team's inactivity window, disqualified, or duplicate |

Rules that keep categories honest:

- A deal moves down automatically when a criterion stops being true (for example, the buyer postpones the signature date past period end).
- Moving a deal up requires new evidence, recorded in the deal notes.
- Managers may adjust a rep's call, but the adjustment is recorded separately so rep and manager accuracy can both be measured.

## MEDDPICC used correctly

MEDDPICC is a qualification framework. Each letter names something the seller must know and be able to prove. The failure mode is treating it as fields to fill rather than facts to verify.

| Element | Correct definition | Evidence that counts | Common misuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metrics | The quantified business outcome the customer expects from buying, in the customer's own numbers | The customer stated or documented the measure and the size of improvement they expect | Seller's generic value claims; metrics the customer never agreed to |
| Economic buyer | The person with authority to approve the spend and overrule a "no" | Direct interaction with that person, who confirmed priority and budget | Assuming a senior title is the economic buyer; "we met them once at kickoff" |
| Decision criteria | The formal and informal criteria the customer will use to choose | Written requirements, evaluation scorecard, or criteria confirmed by the champion and economic buyer | Criteria the seller hopes they use |
| Decision process | The steps, people, and timeline to reach a decision | A described sequence with named people and dates, confirmed by the buyer | "They will decide by end of month" with no steps |
| Paper process | The steps from decision to signature: legal review, procurement, security review, signature authority | Named contacts in legal and procurement, known review durations, contract version in circulation | Discovered only after verbal agreement, causing slippage |
| Identify pain | The business problem driving the purchase and the cost of not solving it | Customer-stated consequence of inaction, linked to a timeline | Technical wishes with no business consequence |
| Champion | A person inside the buyer with power and influence who is actively selling on the seller's behalf and has a personal stake in success | Champion has taken a testable action: arranged access to the economic buyer, shared internal information, or advocated in a meeting the seller did not attend | Confusing a friendly contact or a coach (someone who gives information but does not sell internally) with a champion |
| Competition | Every alternative the buyer is considering, including doing nothing, building internally, or using budget elsewhere | Buyer-confirmed list of alternatives and where the seller stands | Listing only named vendors; ignoring "do nothing" |

### Inspection questions per element

Ask the owner for evidence, not opinions:

- **Metrics:** "What number did the customer say this will move, and where is that written?"
- **Economic buyer:** "When did you last speak with the economic buyer, and what did they say about timing and budget?"
- **Decision criteria:** "Which criteria have we met, and who confirmed it?"
- **Decision process:** "What are the remaining steps, who owns each, and on what dates?"
- **Paper process:** "Who in legal and procurement has the contract, and how long do their reviews usually take?"
- **Pain:** "What happens to the customer if they do not buy this period?"
- **Champion:** "What has the champion done for us that cost them something?"
- **Competition:** "What is the buyer's alternative if we lose, including doing nothing?"

### Scoring for the forecast

Score each element as **Verified** (evidence exists), **Claimed** (owner asserts it, no evidence), or **Unknown**. Report gaps rather than an overall percentage; a deal with an unverified economic buyer and unmapped paper process is a commit risk regardless of how many other elements are verified. For commit, economic buyer, decision process, and paper process should be Verified at minimum; adapt the minimum set with the sales leader and write it into the commit criteria.

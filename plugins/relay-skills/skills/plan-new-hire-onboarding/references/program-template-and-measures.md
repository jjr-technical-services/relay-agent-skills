# Reusable onboarding program template and success measures

## Team program template

A team template standardizes what is common to every hire and leaves prompts for the role-specific parts. Structure:

**1. Program owner and roles**
- Program owner (keeps the template current).
- Hiring manager responsibilities.
- Buddy responsibilities and time commitment.
- HR, IT, and system-owner responsibilities.

**2. Standard pre-start checklist** (same for every hire, with owners and lead times).

**3. Standard day one and week one** (agenda blocks with the role-specific parts marked "[role-specific]").

**4. Role module prompts**
- First-year outcomes for this role: [manager fills].
- Definition of productive: [manager fills].
- 30/60/90 outcomes: [manager fills; three to five each].
- First meaningful contribution: [choose from the team's candidate list].
- Role-specific systems and access: [list with approvers].
- Stakeholder map: [people and purpose].
- Learning plan topics: [role-specific topics plus team standard topics].

**5. Cadence and checkpoint questions** (standard).

**6. Feedback survey** (standard, with the same questions every time so results are comparable).

**7. Maintenance**: reviewed after every few hires or at a fixed interval; broken links, stale documents, and access steps that failed are fixed.

## Manager's checklist

**Before start**
- [ ] 30/60/90 draft written with role-specific outcomes.
- [ ] First meaningful contribution chosen and prepared.
- [ ] Equipment and all access requested with lead times checked.
- [ ] Buddy assigned and briefed; buddy's time agreed with their manager.
- [ ] First-week calendar built, including introductions with a purpose.
- [ ] Welcome message sent; team told who is joining and when.

**Day one**
- [ ] Welcome conversation; walk through the 30/60/90 draft.
- [ ] Confirm equipment and access work; escalate anything broken the same day.
- [ ] Buddy introduced.
- [ ] One small real task completed.

**Week one**
- [ ] Required training scheduled.
- [ ] First contribution started with a reviewer.
- [ ] End-of-week one-to-one: outcomes confirmed, questions answered, onboarding feedback collected.

**Days 30, 60, 90**
- [ ] Checkpoint held; outcomes assessed with evidence.
- [ ] Plan adjusted where support, access, or scope was the problem.
- [ ] Feedback survey sent (if the team uses one).
- [ ] At day 90, transition to the normal performance and development cycle.

## Success measures

Define each measure before collecting it, and use the same definitions across hires.

| Measure | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first contribution | Days from start date to completion of the first meaningful contribution | Easy to measure; early signal |
| Time to productivity | Days from start date until the new hire meets the role's definition of productive, as judged by the manager against the stated definition | Role-specific; compare only within the same role |
| 30/60/90 outcome attainment | Share of outcomes met at each checkpoint | Look at which outcomes are missed across hires; often a plan or access issue |
| Access readiness | Share of required accounts working on day one | A direct measure of pre-start execution |
| New-hire feedback | Scores and comments from a short survey at fixed points (for example, end of week one and day 90) | Keep questions constant; include "What would have helped you get productive faster?" |
| Early retention | Share of hires still employed at a fixed point, such as 6 or 12 months | Small teams will have too few hires for meaningful rates; review cases individually |
| Manager assessment | Manager's view at day 90 of readiness against expectations | Pair with the new hire's own view |

Interpreting measures:

- With few hires, treat measures as case notes, not statistics. Avoid comparing teams on small numbers.
- Time to productivity depends heavily on the role and on how productive is defined; do not compare across roles.
- If feedback repeatedly mentions the same obstacle (access delays, missing documentation, unclear priorities), fix the program rather than coaching individual hires to cope.
- Do not use the feedback survey to evaluate the new hire; keep it about the program, and consider making it anonymous where numbers allow.

## Sample feedback questions

- On day one, did you have the equipment and access you needed? (Yes / Partly / No, with comment)
- How clear are you on what success looks like at 30, 60, and 90 days? (scale)
- How useful were your introductions? Which were missing?
- How useful has your buddy been? (scale, comment)
- What would have helped you get productive faster?
- What should we stop doing in onboarding?

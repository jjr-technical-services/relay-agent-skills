# Writing role-specific 30/60/90 outcomes and choosing the first contribution

## Work backward from the role

1. **First-year outcomes.** What must this role have delivered by the end of year one? Take these from the hiring manager and the job description. Example forms: "owns the monthly close for two entities", "runs the paid search program", "owns renewals for a book of accounts", "maintains the billing service".
2. **Definition of productive.** The point at which the person does the core work of the role independently at the level hired. State it concretely for the role. This is what "time to productivity" measures.
3. **Day 90.** Usually close to the definition of productive for roles with short ramp-up, or a defined milestone toward it for roles with long ramp-up (for example, enterprise sales, where deal cycles may exceed 90 days).
4. **Day 60.** Doing the core work with review or support.
5. **Day 30.** Understands the context, has the tools, has completed the first contribution, and has started core work with close support.

## What makes an outcome good

- **Observable**: someone can see whether it happened. "Understands our customers" is not observable; "has joined eight customer calls and presented a summary of the three most common problems to the team" is.
- **Role-specific**: tied to this role's real work. If an outcome would fit any role ("has met the team"), it belongs in the program checklist, not the 30/60/90 outcomes.
- **Checkable**: say how it will be checked and by whom.
- **Achievable with the support planned**: if an outcome depends on access, data, or introductions, those must be in the plan.
- **Few**: three to five outcomes per checkpoint. More becomes a task list.

## Examples by role family (illustrative, adapt all specifics)

| Role | Day 30 | Day 60 | Day 90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Software engineer | Development environment working; first change shipped to production through the normal review process; can explain the architecture of the team's main service | Has delivered a small feature end to end with review; has joined the on-call rotation as shadow | Delivers features independently at the expected level; on-call as primary with backup |
| Account executive | Completed product and sales process training; shadowed discovery and demo calls; pipeline of assigned accounts reviewed with manager | Running own discovery calls; first opportunities qualified with the team's qualification method | Pipeline built to the level the manager sets; first deals progressing through stages; forecast submitted on time |
| Financial analyst | Has access to the ledger and reporting tools; has reproduced last month's variance report with guidance | Prepares the monthly variance report with review; answers budget-holder questions | Owns the monthly report and a forecast area; presents findings to a budget holder |
| Marketing manager | Audited the current program they will own; documented how results are measured | Runs one campaign or test end to end with review | Owns the program; reports results against agreed metrics and proposes next quarter's plan |
| Support specialist | Completed product training; handled simple tickets with review | Handles standard tickets independently within the team's quality standard | Handles the full ticket range for their tier; contributes to a help article |

These examples show the pattern (context, supported work, independent work). The actual outcomes must come from the team's real work and standards.

## Choosing the first meaningful contribution

Criteria:

- **Real**: it matters to someone other than the new hire. Not an invented exercise.
- **Bounded**: can be completed in days, not weeks.
- **Touches the core tools and process**: shipping a change, closing a ticket, publishing an asset, updating a real report.
- **Has a reviewer**: the buddy or a teammate who will give feedback quickly.
- **Visible**: the team sees it, so the new hire is recognized early.

Keep a short list of candidate contributions on the team backlog, labelled for new hires, so the manager is not inventing one the week before a start date.

## Learning plan

For each topic:

- Why it matters to the role.
- Source: a document, a recording, a person, a system, a customer call.
- Order: learn what is needed for the first contribution first; defer deep topics.
- Proof of learning: the new hire uses it in real work, explains it back, or completes a check.

Common topic areas: the product or service; customers and their problems; the team's goals and metrics; processes and decision-making; systems and data; domain knowledge; company policies relevant to the role.

Avoid a reading list with no purpose. Documents are useful when tied to a task the new hire is doing that week.

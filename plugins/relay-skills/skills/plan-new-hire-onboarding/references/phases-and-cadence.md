# Onboarding phases, checklists, and cadence

## Pre-start (from signed offer to day one)

The goal is that nothing on day one depends on a request that could have been made earlier.

| Item | Typical owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Employment paperwork and payroll setup | HR or people operations | Follow the company's process and local requirements; do not improvise |
| Right-to-work or eligibility verification | HR | Timing rules vary by country; HR decides |
| Equipment (laptop, peripherals, phone if needed) | IT or operations | Order early; shipping to remote hires takes longer |
| Core accounts (email, identity provider, chat, calendar) | IT | Must work on day one |
| Role-specific system access | System owners | List each system and its approver; many need manager approval and have their own lead time |
| Buddy assigned and briefed | Manager | Choose someone who does similar work, is not the manager, and has capacity |
| First-week calendar holds | Manager | Introductions, training, one-to-ones, team rituals |
| Welcome message | Manager | What to expect on day one, where to go or which link to join, who will meet them |
| 30/60/90 draft | Manager | Shared on day one, refined together in week one |
| Team announcement | Manager | Name, role, start date, what they will work on |
| First contribution prepared | Manager and buddy | Task defined, context documents gathered, reviewer named |

Keep pre-start contact welcoming and light. Do not assign work before the start date.

## Day one

Principles: working tools, a human welcome, clarity about the first week, and one small real accomplishment.

A sample shape (adapt to the team):

- Start: manager welcome, day-one agenda, and a walk through the 30/60/90 draft.
- Setup: equipment and access check with IT; resolve anything broken immediately.
- Buddy: introduction, tour of the team's tools and where information lives.
- Team: brief introductions at a team meeting or lunch.
- Required: only the administration that must happen on day one.
- Real task: something small and completable (for example, update a document, fix a small issue, shadow a customer call and write the notes).
- Close: manager check-in, questions, plan for day two.

## Week one

- **Introductions with purpose**: each meeting states why the new hire should know this person, what they will work on together, and one question to ask.
- **Required training**: scheduled, not left to find time.
- **Context**: product or service overview, customers, team goals and current priorities, how decisions are made, where documentation lives.
- **Tools**: working setup of every core tool, verified by doing a real task in each.
- **Shadowing**: watching real work (calls, reviews, planning, support queue).
- **First contribution**: started, with the buddy as first reviewer.
- **End of week one**: manager one-to-one to confirm the 30/60/90 outcomes, discuss what is confusing, and collect onboarding feedback.

## Weeks two to twelve

- Increasing ownership: from shadowing, to doing with review, to doing independently.
- Learning plan topics scheduled across weeks, not front-loaded.
- Stakeholder introductions continue as the new hire's work reaches new groups.
- Formal checkpoints at 30, 60, and 90 days.

## Cadence

| Touchpoint | Frequency in first 90 days | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Manager one-to-one | Weekly is common practice; more often in week one | Priorities, feedback both ways, obstacles, progress on outcomes |
| Buddy check-in | Several times in week one, then weekly or as needed | Informal questions, norms, "how things really work" |
| 30-day checkpoint | Once | Review 30-day outcomes; adjust the plan |
| 60-day checkpoint | Once | Review progress; confirm the 90-day outcomes are still right |
| 90-day checkpoint | Once | Assess against outcomes; decide on any support needed; transition to the normal performance cycle |
| Onboarding feedback survey | At end of week one and at day 90 (or a similar cadence the team chooses) | Improve the program |

Checkpoint conversation questions:

- Which outcomes are met, partly met, not yet met? What evidence?
- What has helped most? What has slowed you down?
- What do you understand now that you wish you had known on day one?
- What support do you need for the next 30 days?
- (Manager to self) What have I not provided that I committed to?

If outcomes are not on track at 30 or 60 days, first check whether the plan, the access, or the support was the problem before concluding it is the person.

## The buddy role

- Not the manager; ideally a peer who does similar work.
- Answers questions the new hire may hesitate to ask the manager.
- Reviews the first contribution.
- Introduces the new hire to informal norms and people.
- Time commitment agreed with the buddy's manager; buddying is real work and should be recognized.

# Post-award tracking, variances, and budget modifications

## Budget-to-actual report

Produce monthly for internal use and on the funder's schedule for reporting.

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Line | Budget line or funder category |
| Approved budget | Current approved amount, including approved modifications |
| Actual to date | Posted expenses coded to the grant through the period end |
| Expected to date | Budget phased to the same point (by activity timing, not straight-line unless costs are even) |
| Variance to date | Actual to date − expected to date |
| Variance % | Variance ÷ expected to date |
| Remaining | Approved budget − actual to date |
| Projected at end | Actual to date + forecast of remaining spend |
| Projected variance | Projected at end − approved budget |
| Note | Cause and action for any line beyond threshold |

Compare against a phased expectation. A line that is 50% spent halfway through the period is not automatically on track if its costs were scheduled for the second half.

## Variance thresholds

Funders set thresholds for when a variance must be explained or approved. They differ, so extract the grant agreement's wording. Patterns seen in agreements include a percentage of a line or category, a percentage of the total award, or a fixed amount. Record the exact rule in the tracking plan.

For US federal awards, 2 CFR 200.308 sets the circumstances that need prior approval, including a change in scope or objectives and a change in key personnel named in the award, and it allows the agency to require prior approval for cumulative transfers among direct cost categories above 10% of the total budget as last approved (where the federal share exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold). Confirm the current text and the award's own terms, which can be stricter.

When the funder sets no threshold, use an internal one and label it as the organization's choice (for example, explain any line off by more than a set percentage and a set amount, whichever the finance lead picks), so small lines do not generate noise and large lines are never silent.

## Explaining a variance

For each variance beyond threshold, write:

1. **What**: line, amount, percentage.
2. **Cause**: timing (spend moved between periods), volume (more or fewer activities), rate (unit cost changed), or scope (activity added or dropped).
3. **Effect on deliverables**: none, delayed, reduced, or expanded.
4. **Action**: no action, re-phase, request a modification, or cover from unrestricted funds.

Keep causes factual. "Underspent" with no cause invites a question; "Personnel underspent by 9,000 because the coordinator started in month 4 instead of month 1; workshops were rescheduled into months 5 to 12 and the annual target is unchanged" answers it. General variance commentary for the whole organization belongs with budget-variance-explainer; grant-report narratives belong with write-funder-report.

## Budget modification requests

Request a modification when a projected variance crosses the funder's threshold, when scope or key personnel change, or when an unspent balance needs to move to a new category or a no-cost extension.

A request contains:

- award identifier, period, and program officer;
- a table of current approved budget, proposed budget, and change by line;
- the reason for each change tied to the program (not "we have money left");
- the effect on objectives and deliverables, stated honestly;
- confirmation that the change keeps within the indirect cap and match commitment, recalculated with the new figures;
- the date by which a decision is needed.

Send the request before the spending happens. Costs incurred on a changed plan before approval risk being disallowed.

## Close-out

- Reconcile final actuals to the ledger and to the last report.
- Confirm match was met and documented.
- Return or request carry-forward of unspent funds per the agreement.
- Keep records for the funder's required retention period (for federal awards, generally three years from the final financial report under 2 CFR 200.334, with exceptions).

# Trends, content insights, and attribution

## Period-over-period checklist

Run these before describing any change as a trend.

1. **Denominator change**: did list size, send count, reach, or posting volume change?
   A rate can move because the base moved.
2. **Measurement change**: new privacy features, a platform redefining reach or
   engagement, tracking or consent-banner changes, a new email tool, list cleaning.
3. **Small base**: counts below the stable-base threshold (the organization's number;
   commonly used defaults of 30 or so are heuristics, not rules) can swing widely by
   chance. Report the raw counts and call it too small to judge.
4. **One-off events**: a news story, a campaign, a partner repost, a holiday, an
   awareness month, a send-time change.
5. **Seasonality**: compare with the same month last year when available; giving and
   academic-year audiences are strongly seasonal.
6. **Mix change**: more video, a different topic, a new audience segment.

If a change survives the checklist, say "up" or "down" plainly and give the number. If
it does not, say what would need to be true to call it.

## Content-level insights

- Rank content by the metric tied to its goal (registrations for an event post, clicks
  for a resource post, engagement rate for awareness content), not by reach.
- Group by topic, format, channel, time of day, and call to action. Report each pattern
  with the number of posts behind it.
- Label an observation from one or two posts as a hypothesis and suggest a way to test
  it next month (repeat the format, alternate topics, or run an A/B test with
  ab-test-setup when volume allows).
- Look at negative signals too: unsubscribes after a send, hidden posts, negative
  comments, drops in list growth.

## Attribution caveats

| Situation | What the number shows | What it cannot show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Donation platform credits the last clicked link | Where the donor came from immediately before giving | Whether the email or post made them give; earlier touches; offline influence |
| Campaign parameters on links | Which link was used | People who saw the post and gave later by another route |
| Self-reported "how did you hear about us" | The donor's memory | Precise channel credit; memory favors recent or memorable touches |
| Platform-reported conversions (ad tools) | The platform's own attribution window and rules | Whether the gift would have happened anyway |
| Year-end or matching-gift periods | Gifts during a high-intent season | How much communications added beyond the season |

Write attribution as "gifts credited to the newsletter by last click" rather than "gifts
from the newsletter". For "did it work" questions, hand off to
measure-marketing-incrementality, which designs holdouts and other tests.

## Writing insights

Each insight has four parts: what happened (with numbers), the evidence and its size,
the likely explanation labelled as an interpretation, and what to do about it.

Illustrative (hypothetical): "Posts featuring a how-to resource averaged a 6.0 percent
engagement rate by reach across 4 posts, compared with 3.0 percent across 8 other posts.
Four posts is a small set, so treat this as a hypothesis. Next month: publish three
resource posts and compare."

## Recommendations

- Three to five actions, each with goal, owner, and success measure.
- At least one measurement fix when a caveat blocked a conclusion (for example, switch
  the email trend to click rate, add campaign parameters to all donate links, record
  the same engagement definition every month).
- Stop doing something when the evidence supports it; plans that only add work are
  not followed.

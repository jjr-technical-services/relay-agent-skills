Use this standard for a comprehensive deliverable. Compress sections only when the user asks for a brief.

## 1. Status and scope

State:

- offering and business model;
- market, geography, product state, channel, and decision horizon;
- decision owner and intended use;
- research period and sources reviewed;
- status: `hypothesis`, `provisional`, `evidence-backed`, or `validated for stated scope`.

Define what the deliverable does not establish. In particular, distinguish analysis, message testing, product-market fit, commercial validation, and production evidence.

## 2. Executive recommendation

Lead with:

- primary ICP in one sentence;
- primary trigger and job;
- recommended UVP in one or two sentences;
- why this segment wins now;
- largest evidence gap;
- next decision or test.

Avoid an executive summary that merely repeats section headings.

## 3. Evidence register

Summarize source coverage and contradictions. Include:

| Evidence area | Best evidence | State | Strength | Limitation | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Name missing evidence that could reverse the recommendation. Put citations or source references next to claims, not only in a bibliography.

## 4. Segment decision

Provide a comparable scorecard:

| Rank | Segment | Raw | Confidence-adjusted | Evidence confidence | Knockout | Decision |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|

Follow with the decisive tradeoffs, sensitivity to weights or assumptions, and the runner-up case. If scores are not appropriate, explain the qualitative decision rule used.

## 5. Primary ICP

### Definition

Write a recognition rule, not a portrait:

> [Customer/account] in [specific situation] with [observable problem/trigger], [required fit], and [economic/access condition], excluding [material disqualifiers].

### Account or customer

Include required and attractive characteristics separately:

- operating context and relevant scale;
- workflow, technology, regulation, or environment;
- economics and cost of inaction;
- readiness and required capability;
- reachability and channel;
- time-to-value and delivery fit.

### Buying committee

| Role | Typical title/identity | Goal | Concern | Decision criterion | Proof needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover champion, economic buyer, user, evaluator, approver, blocker, procurement, and beneficiary only where relevant.

### User and job

Describe:

- job or progress sought;
- situation and trigger;
- current workflow and alternatives;
- functional, emotional, and social stakes;
- frequency and consequence;
- adoption friction and switching cost;
- desired outcome and measurement.

### Qualification

List:

- positive signals;
- required signals;
- discovery questions;
- red flags;
- disqualifiers;
- re-entry conditions.

## 6. Secondary and anti-ICP

For each secondary or experimental segment, state why it is not primary, what investment is appropriate, and what evidence would promote or remove it.

For the anti-ICP, state observable characteristics, expected harm or poor economics, routing or response, and any ethical or legal limitation on the exclusion rule.

## 7. UVP and message architecture

Include:

### Positioning statement

Use target, problem/trigger, category, outcome, alternative, mechanism, and proof.

### Customer-facing UVP

Use one concise primary promise. Add a subhead only when needed to explain mechanism, audience, or proof.

### Value pillars

| Pillar | Customer value | Mechanism | Proof | Objection answered |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Limit the main hierarchy to three pillars. Move features beneath the pillar they support.

### Role or channel adaptations

Adapt only the formats requested. Label draft copy separately from substantiated claims.

## 8. Claim-proof ledger

Use:

| ID | Claim | State | Evidence | Scope/qualification | Owner | Next proof | Publication risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Call out claims that must not ship. If the user supplied proof but its use rights are unclear, flag permission as an open dependency.

## 9. Validation plan

Prioritize the assumptions that are both uncertain and decision-critical:

| Priority | Hypothesis | Segment | Method | Sample | Success threshold | Failure threshold | Decision rule | Owner |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|

Include leading and lagging indicators. Define the time window. Note confounders and whether the test measures attention, intent, commitment, adoption, value, retention, or economics.

## 10. Handoff and revision

End with:

- decisions made;
- open decisions;
- owners and dependencies;
- evidence collection plan;
- review date or revision trigger;
- where the source-of-truth artifact lives.

Keep these states separate:

- drafted;
- internally approved;
- externally tested;
- behaviorally validated;
- commercially validated;
- adopted in product or go-to-market execution.

A completed deliverable is not proof that the organization adopted or validated it.

## 1. Positioning before copy

Resolve five choices before writing a headline:

1. **Target:** The selected ICP and buying situation.
2. **Problem/job:** The consequential progress the customer is trying to make.
3. **Alternative:** The real status quo or competing approach.
4. **Distinct value:** The outcome that matters more for this ICP.
5. **Mechanism and proof:** Why the offering can credibly produce the outcome.

Keep internal positioning precise even when external language is short. Category framing can help customers understand the offering, but do not accept a category label that hides the differentiated value.

## 2. Value architecture

Build a benefit ladder for each candidate promise:

| Layer | Question | Example form |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | What does the offering do? | Automates intake across channels |
| Mechanism | How does it work differently? | Uses one governed workflow and shared context |
| Functional outcome | What task improves? | Fewer handoffs and faster resolution |
| Business outcome | What changes economically or operationally? | Lower service cost and recovered capacity |
| Emotional/social outcome | How does the stakeholder feel or appear? | Confident the operation is under control |
| Proof | Why should the customer believe it? | Measured cycle-time reduction in comparable deployments |

Lead with the highest outcome the evidence can support. Use the mechanism and proof to make the promise credible.

## 3. Differentiation tests

A differentiator is useful only if it passes all four tests:

- **Relevant:** The selected ICP cares enough for it to affect the decision.
- **Distinct:** The real alternative does not deliver it equally well.
- **Provable:** Evidence can support the comparison and scope.
- **Durable:** It is not merely a generic feature or easy claim with no defensible system behind it.

Classify proposed differentiation:

- product or technical mechanism;
- data, network, or learning advantage;
- workflow or integration advantage;
- service, delivery, or expertise advantage;
- economic or business-model advantage;
- trust, compliance, or risk advantage;
- focus or specialization advantage.

Avoid "easy," "seamless," "innovative," "powerful," "all-in-one," "best-in-class," and "AI-powered" unless the words are operationally defined and supported.

## 4. UVP forms

### Internal positioning

> For [specific ICP] who [job/problem] when [trigger], [offering] is a [category/frame] that [primary outcome]. Unlike [alternative], it [differentiated mechanism], supported by [proof].

### One-line value proposition

> [Achieve concrete outcome] for [specific customer or situation] without [cost, risk, or friction of the alternative].

Use this only when the mechanism or proof appears nearby. Do not force the template when it produces unnatural language.

### Message hierarchy

- **Primary promise:** One outcome that organizes the story.
- **Pillar 1:** Most important functional or economic value.
- **Pillar 2:** Differentiating mechanism or experience.
- **Pillar 3:** Risk reduction, adoption, or proof.
- **Reason to believe:** Specific evidence for every pillar.
- **Objection response:** Acknowledge the concern and answer with mechanism or proof.

### Sales hypothesis

> When [trigger] happens, [role] struggles to [job] because [constraint]. We help [ICP] [outcome] by [mechanism], and the evidence to test first is [proof].

Use this for discovery; do not present it as a memorized claim that replaces listening.

## 5. Proof and claim governance

Maintain this ledger:

| Claim | Audience | Baseline | Evidence | State | Qualification | Risk | Next proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Claim states:

- `substantiated`: sufficient evidence for the exact scope and wording;
- `directionally supported`: useful evidence, but scope or sample limits the claim;
- `hypothesis`: plausible and testable, not publishable as fact;
- `unsupported`: remove or replace;
- `prohibited`: legally, ethically, or contractually unsuitable.

Check quantitative claims for sample, baseline, period, population, methodology, and whether the result is typical. Check comparative claims against the named alternative. Preserve disclaimers where outcomes depend on implementation, customer behavior, or external conditions.

Do not use confidential customer data, names, logos, or quotations without authorization. Do not create health, financial, employment, safety, or legal outcome claims from general product evidence.

## 6. Message adaptation

Adapt the same positioning without changing the underlying promise:

- **Executive buyer:** Business outcome, risk, time-to-value, and proof.
- **Champion:** Workflow improvement, internal case, and adoption path.
- **User:** Immediate job, friction removed, and experience.
- **Technical evaluator:** Mechanism, architecture, compatibility, and controls.
- **Procurement/legal:** Scope, governance, support, and risk allocation.
- **Landing page:** Clear audience, promise, mechanism, proof, objection, and action.
- **Outbound:** Trigger relevance and a credible reason for the conversation.
- **Product onboarding:** First value and expectation-setting.

Do not create different positioning for each channel. Change emphasis and vocabulary while preserving target, promise, mechanism, and proof.

## 7. Audit rubric

Rate an existing UVP from 0-2 on each dimension:

- target specificity;
- problem and trigger clarity;
- outcome importance;
- alternative awareness;
- differentiated mechanism;
- evidence and credibility;
- language clarity;
- consistency with product and delivery reality;
- adaptability across buying roles;
- falsifiability.

Interpretation:

- `17-20`: strong and ready for focused testing;
- `13-16`: promising but materially incomplete;
- `8-12`: generic, unclear, or weakly supported;
- `0-7`: rebuild from ICP and evidence.

Explain the score dimension by dimension. Do not let polished wording compensate for missing target, mechanism, or proof.

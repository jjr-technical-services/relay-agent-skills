# Sources, the 990-PF, and the fact ledger

## Which organizations file what

| Funder type | Public filing | What it shows about grants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private foundation (family, independent, most corporate foundations) | IRS Form 990-PF | Every grant paid in the year, with recipient, purpose and amount; grants approved for future payment; application instructions |
| Public charity that makes grants (community foundations, some intermediaries) | IRS Form 990, Schedule I | Grants over 5,000 to any one domestic organization, with recipient, amount, and purpose |
| Corporate giving program run from company funds (not a foundation) | Usually nothing public | Only what the company publishes |
| Donor-advised fund | Grants appear under the sponsoring organization's Schedule I, not the donor's name | Rarely traceable to the individual donor |
| Government funder | Published notice of funding opportunity and award lists | Eligibility, priorities, scoring criteria, award ranges |

Where to find returns: the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search, ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer, and Candid's databases publish filed returns. Use whichever the user can access; cite the return itself (form, tax year, part), not the website that hosted it.

## Reading a 990-PF for prospect research

Work through these parts. Part numbers have shifted between form revisions, so cite the part by its title as well as its number for the tax year you read.

1. **Header**: foundation name, employer identification number (EIN), address, tax year. The tax year is the source date for everything on the return.
2. **Revenue and expenses (Part I)**: the line for contributions, gifts, and grants paid gives total giving for the year. Compare two or three years to see whether giving is rising, flat, or falling.
3. **Balance sheet (Part II)**: fair market value of assets at year end. Private foundations generally must distribute about 5% of the average value of their non-charitable-use assets each year, so assets indicate capacity, but the grants list shows behaviour. Do not estimate a grant size from assets.
4. **Information about officers, directors, trustees, and foundation managers**: names for relationship mapping against the organization's board and network. Treat any overlap as a lead to confirm, never as a relationship.
5. **Supplementary information: application process**: contact, form of application, deadlines, and restrictions. It also has a box the foundation checks if it "only makes contributions to preselected charitable organizations" and does not accept unsolicited requests for funds; when that box is checked, classify access as "Invitation only" unless the funder's current website says otherwise.
6. **Supplementary information: grants and contributions paid during the year**: recipient, relationship (if any) to foundation managers, the recipient's foundation status, purpose, and amount. This is the most valuable list on the return.
7. **Grants approved for future payment**: multi-year commitments already made, which reduce new capacity.

From the grants list, calculate for comparable recipients (same field, same geography, similar budget):

- count of grants;
- median and range of grant amounts (use the median rather than the mean, because one large grant distorts the average);
- share of grants that repeat to the same recipient across years (a high repeat share means few openings for new grantees);
- share described as general support versus a named project;
- any grants to peer organizations, which support the "recent giving" score and point to a possible introduction.

Illustrative (hypothetical round numbers): a return lists 40 grants, 30 of them to organizations that also appear on the previous year's return. The repeat share is 30 ÷ 40 = 75%, which leaves about 10 grants a year open to anyone new, before considering fit.

## Other sources and what each is good for

- **Funder website and guidelines**: current priorities, eligibility, geography, process, deadlines. Most authoritative for anything forward-looking. Record the page title and the date retrieved.
- **Annual reports and grants databases on the funder's site**: often more current than the 990-PF and sometimes list grant purposes in more detail.
- **Peer organizations' annual reports and donor walls**: show who funds similar work nearby. A funder appearing on three peers' lists is a stronger lead than one appearing in a generic directory.
- **CRM history**: prior applications, declines with reasons, and who knows whom. A decline two years ago with the reason "outside current priorities" is evidence; record it.
- **Program officer conversations**: record date, person, and what was said. Do not paraphrase an encouraging conversation into a commitment.
- **Directories and paid databases**: good for discovery; confirm every fact against the funder's own materials or return before relying on it.

## Staleness rules

- Default threshold (an assumption the user can change): facts older than 18 months from their source date are "Stale - re-verify".
- A 990-PF is filed up to several months after the tax year ends (with extensions, later still), so the newest public return is often one to two years old. Treat its grant list as history, not as the funder's current strategy.
- Guidelines pages change without notice. Record the retrieval date and re-check before any application.
- Leadership or strategy changes (a new executive director, a strategic plan announcement) make earlier giving patterns less predictive; note them.

## Resolving conflicts between sources

1. The funder's current guidelines or a program officer's written statement.
2. The funder's most recent annual report or grants database.
3. The most recent filed return.
4. Peer materials.
5. Third-party directories.

When two sources conflict, keep both in the fact ledger with status "Conflicting", use the higher-ranked one for scoring, and list the question to confirm.

## Fact ledger format

| Funder | Fact | Source | Source date | Retrieved | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example Foundation (hypothetical) | Funds youth programs in two named counties | Funder guidelines page, "Eligibility" section | Undated page | Date retrieved | Current |
| Example Foundation (hypothetical) | Median grant to youth organizations 20,000 across 12 grants | 990-PF, grants paid, tax year shown on return | Tax year end | Date retrieved | Stale - re-verify |

Every fact used in the scorecard needs a row. A fact without a row is removed from the scorecard.

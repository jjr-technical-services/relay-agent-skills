# Prospect pipeline and cultivation

## Stages and exit criteria

| Stage | What happens | Exit criterion to move on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify | Name found from a source; no facts checked | At least mission, geography, and access facts entered in the fact ledger |
| Qualify | Facts gathered and scored; knockouts applied | Recommendation is Pursue now or Cultivate first, with evidence coverage above the minimum |
| Cultivate | Relationship built: introduction, information session, site visit, program officer call | Funder confirms interest or invites an application, or the process is open and the fit is confirmed |
| LOI | Letter of inquiry or pre-application submitted (drafted with the grant-letter-of-inquiry prompt) | Invitation to a full proposal, or a decline |
| Full proposal | Application prepared and submitted (write-grant-proposal, build-grant-budget) | Submitted before the deadline |
| Decision | Awaiting and receiving the decision | Award, decline, or deferral recorded with reason |
| Steward | Award managed and reported (write-funder-report); relationship maintained | Renewal decision, or a planned exit |

A decline goes back to Qualify with the reason recorded; do not reapply to the same funder with the same request unless the reason has changed.

## Next actions

Every active prospect has exactly one next action with an owner and a date. Good next actions are concrete and small:

- "Confirm the application cycle on the funder's current guidelines page."
- "Ask the board chair whether she knows the trustee named on the return."
- "Register for the funder's information session."
- "Request a 20-minute call with the program officer to test fit for general operating support."

Weak next actions to avoid: "Look into it", "Reach out", "Follow up".

## Cultivation for funders classed Invitation only

1. Confirm the Invitation only status from the funder's current materials; returns can lag.
2. Look for an honest route: a board or staff member who knows a trustee, a peer grantee willing to introduce, a convening the funder hosts, or a public program officer contact for inquiries.
3. Offer information the funder can use (a short program summary, a research brief, an invitation to see the program), not a funding request.
4. Record each touch in the CRM with the date and outcome.
5. Wait for the funder to invite an application. An unsolicited proposal to a funder that says it does not accept them usually wastes effort and can harm the relationship.

## Capacity and calendar check

Before committing to a quarter's pipeline:

- list published deadlines and estimate preparation hours per application from the organization's own history;
- compare total hours with available staff time;
- drop or defer the lowest-ranked prospects until the plan fits;
- account for reporting load on current awards, which competes for the same people.

Illustrative (hypothetical round numbers): six applications at an estimated 30 hours each need 180 hours; if the development lead has 120 hours available this quarter for proposals, two applications must move to the next quarter or get help.

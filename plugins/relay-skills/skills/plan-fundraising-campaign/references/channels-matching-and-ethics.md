# Channels, matching gifts and ethics

## Channel plan and cadence

Build a calendar with one row per touch. Principles:

- **Leadership first.** Ask leadership prospects personally before public launch so the campaign opens with committed gifts.
- **Suppress donors from reminders.** Once someone gives, move them to the thank-you track. Asking a donor again in the same campaign is a common, avoidable complaint.
- **Match channel to consent.** Email to people on the email list; postal mail to people with an address who have not opted out; text messages only to people who gave express consent to receive texts from the organization (counsel confirms the consent standard); social posts to the public.
- **Coordinate across teams.** Check newsletters, program emails and event invitations in the same window so no one receives several asks in a few days.
- **Cadence is chosen, not copied.** State the number of touches per segment and why. Stop rules: no more reminders after a donor gives, unsubscribes or asks to pause.

Channel roles, for choosing rather than prescribing:

| Channel | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Personal ask (visit, call) | Leadership and major gifts | Requires preparation and a named asker |
| Postal letter | Older and loyal donors, year-end | Lead time for printing and mail |
| Email | Launch, updates, reminders to non-donors | List hygiene, one clear ask per email |
| Text message | Short reminders to consenting supporters | Consent, frequency, opt-out handling |
| Social | Reach, peer-to-peer amplification | Stories need consent; comments need moderation |
| Peer-to-peer pages | New donors through fundraisers' networks | Fundraiser coaching and approved language |
| Workplace channels | Employee giving | Company approval for internal promotion |

## Matching gift mechanics

A **match** is a commitment by one donor to give an amount tied to other donors' gifts (for example 1:1 up to a cap). A **challenge** is a gift released only if a target is reached.

Record in writing:

- matcher name and whether they consent to public attribution (or wish to be anonymous);
- ratio (1:1, 2:1) and cap;
- eligible gifts (all gifts, new donors only, increased gifts only, recurring gifts first installment) and eligible dates;
- what happens if the cap is not reached: does the matcher give the full amount anyway, give only the matched amount, or something else;
- whether the matcher's gift is restricted.

Truthful disclosure: donors are told the actual terms. If the matcher will give the full amount regardless, do not say that gifts "unlock" the match or that the match will be "lost". Say, for example, that every gift up to the cap will be matched, if that is what the matcher agreed. Track match progress from actual eligible gifts, not pledges.

Corporate matching gifts for employees are separate: the employee submits a match request under the company's policy, and the organization confirms the gift. Track submitted, confirmed and paid matches, and follow up before the company's deadline.

## Ethics rules

Run this check on every message, page and form before launch:

| Rule | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| No guilt or fear framing | Do not imply a donor is responsible for harm if they do not give. Describe the need and the opportunity. |
| No fake urgency | Deadlines must be real (for example the calendar year end, a match end date the matcher set). No invented "only hours left" pressure. |
| No countdown timers | A date and time are enough. Timers on the organization's own pages pressure rather than inform. |
| No pre-checked recurring gifts | Monthly giving is an explicit choice. The one-time option is the default or neither is pre-selected. |
| No pre-checked fee coverage or add-ons | Optional extras are opt-in. |
| Honest match language | Matches the matcher's written terms. |
| Consent for stories | Written consent for any identifiable person; parental consent and organizational review for minors; strengths-based framing; no rescue narratives; no distress or poverty imagery as a device. |
| Non-clinical claims | Outcomes stated as what was measured (participation, knowledge, self-reported confidence, help-seeking intentions), never as treatment effects. |
| Restriction honesty | If gifts are restricted to a purpose, spend them on it; if the campaign may raise more than the purpose needs, say in advance where excess goes. |
| Donor and participant data separation | Never use program or service data to target donors. |

## Memorial, tribute and safe messaging

- The family leads: written agreement on name, photos, wording, and whether cause of death is mentioned.
- If a death by suicide is mentioned, follow recognized safe messaging practice, such as the Recommendations for Reporting on Suicide and the Framework for Successful Messaging from the National Action Alliance for Suicide Prevention: no method or location details; do not present suicide as a solution or as caused by a single event; no sensational or romanticized language; include hope and help-seeking; say "died by suicide", not "committed suicide".
- Include the organization's approved crisis resource copy verbatim, shown in plans as the plain-text slot "Slot: organization-approved crisis resource" (no square brackets, so downstream tools do not misread it). In the US, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline can be reached by call or text at 988; use the organization's approved wording where one exists. Never invent a crisis line, number or text code.
- Peer-to-peer fundraisers in a memorial campaign receive the same safe messaging guidance and approved language, and someone monitors page comments.
- Tribute notifications go to the person the family designated; amounts are shared only with the donor's agreement.
